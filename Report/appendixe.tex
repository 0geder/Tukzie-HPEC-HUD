\section{Supporting detail}

\subsection*{Raspberry Pi~4 discovery}

The dashboard's resolver (\texttt{DashboardIntegration/sw7\_endpoints.py}) tries, in order: localhost, an explicit override, a fixed address on a planned Ethernet link (10.20.0.1 and 10.20.0.2), the source of a UDP beacon sent by the bridge every 2\,s on port 50808 (ignored if older than 10\,s), \texttt{pi4-camera.local} through mDNS, and the last address that worked (Figure~\ref{fig:pi4-discovery}). A candidate is accepted when it answers with valid JSON within 3\,s; three failed polls start a new search, with the wait doubling to 30\,s. The offscreen tests pass; the wired link has a setup script but its use on the vehicle is not recorded.

\subsection*{Camera colour}

With the NoIR tuning and saturation 1.8, printed cards held about 1\,m from the camera measured as in Table~\ref{tab:colour-cards} in the detector's own frames under indoor light. The three colours are clearly identified; grey and white show a small cool shift. Held together in one frame the cards remained distinct. The dark-pixel correction scales each pixel's saturation from fully grey at a brightest channel of 50 or below to unchanged at 110 and above; it took 84\,ms a frame with Pillow on the Raspberry Pi~4, so it runs only in the live-view thread and the detector's input is unchanged. The colours should be checked again in daylight.

\subsection*{Pothole model}

Run 1 (2 October 2026) fine-tuned YOLO11n, pretrained on COCO, on a public 608-image pothole dataset in Colab: 46 epochs, stopped 15 epochs after the best validation result; mAP@50 59.7\% at 640 pixels (precision 66\%, recall 50\%) and 55.0\% at 320 on the dataset's own test images. Validation loss stopped improving after about 20 epochs, so more varied images were needed, not longer training. On the South African dataset's 605 test images (1,238 potholes) it scored 0.000 at 640, 1280 and 1920 pixels, recall at most 1.2\%; drawing the converted labels on an image confirmed that the label conversion was correct. Of that dataset's 4,337 images (3680$\times$2760 pixels), 1,987 contain 5,029 potholes, with a median size of 100 by 26 pixels, about 17 by 4.5 pixels after reduction to a 640-pixel input, and all centres lie between 48 and 62\% of the image height. Run 2 (6 October) cropped each frame to the band from 42 to 68\% of its height and cut it into three overlapping 1280-pixel tiles, so that a median pothole is about 50 by 13 pixels at the training input; after 60 epochs on a T4 it reached a validation mAP@50 of 0.714 at 640 pixels (precision 81\%, recall 62\%) and 0.566 at 320. These are validation scores, optimistic because validation chose the best epoch, and per tile. On the Raspberry Pi~4, a COCO YOLO11n through NCNN at a 320-pixel input ran at 7.9\,frames/s, 175\,ms median from readout to result (\texttt{CameraDetection/ncnn\_bench.py}).

\subsection*{Dashboard map and navigation}

The street-tile map (\texttt{DashboardIntegration/sw7\_tile\_map.py}) draws OpenStreetMap tiles with Qt's painter under the tile usage policy (at most two requests at a time, an identifying user agent, no bulk download), caches tiles for 30 days, falls back to scaled lower-zoom tiles or the team's vector roads, and follows heading-up at zoom 17. A prefetch tool cached 656 tiles (11.9\,MB) at zoom 11 to 16 around the campus. Routes come from the public OSRM service. A generated replay track and a simulated drive allow the map to be shown indoors; both are labelled on screen (REPLAY, Simulated drive) so that they cannot be taken for real positions, and a real fix stops the simulation. The Raspberry Pi~5 also carries a Hailo-8 accelerator, which did not appear on the PCI Express bus because the external connector is not enabled in its boot configuration.
