\chapter{Results}
\label{chap:results}

This chapter gives the measured results against the requirements of Table~\ref{tab:requirements-mapping}. Unless marked otherwise, every figure was measured on the bench, on the target board and the two Raspberry Pis, between 25 September and 11 October 2026; the raw evidence is indexed in Appendix~\ref{app:evidence}. Results that depend on the vehicle are marked as outstanding where they will go.

\section{Inertial acquisition (R1)}

Table~\ref{tab:res-acquisition} summarises the acquisition results. Both IMUs held 200\,Hz with no dropped samples in every recorded session, including with all subsystems running together on the target board. The only timing fault was the pause at each flash write. With a write every 10\,s (v0.5.0) both IMUs showed a 33 to 40\,ms gap at every write, twelve in two minutes; with logging switched off for the next two minutes the worst gap was 5.9\,ms. With buffered logging (v0.6.0), in a 330\,s run with five flushes, all ten gaps over 10\,ms (37 to 40\,ms, both IMUs at each flush) fell within 3.5\,s after a flush, and the worst gap elsewhere was again 5.9\,ms. Each flush occupied the modem task for 71 to 202\,ms, which does not affect acquisition. R1 is met on the bench.

\section{Calibration (R2)}

Before calibration, the two IMUs on the development board read about 5.7\% above and 2.6\% below standard gravity at rest, about 8\% apart. The factors stored at their first calibration were 0.9773 and 0.9901, after which both read 9.80 to 9.81\,m/s\textsuperscript{2}, agreeing within measurement noise. The factors imply mean magnitudes at the moment of calibration of 10.03 and 9.90\,m/s\textsuperscript{2}, not the readings seen earlier that day, so the uncalibrated readings had shifted between sessions; the likely cause, not confirmed, is that the sensors were moved in between. On the target board the first boot measured 9.44 and 9.57\,m/s\textsuperscript{2} (factors 1.0390 and 1.0245), and with those factors a later session read median resting magnitudes of 9.93 and 10.10\,m/s\textsuperscript{2}, 1.3\% and 2.9\% above gravity. R2 was met immediately after calibration but not in the later session, which confirms that a single factor holds only for the orientation in which it was measured.

\outstanding{recalibration in the mounted position on the vehicle and the resulting resting magnitudes.}

\section{Ride features and fusion (R3)}

The feature self-test passed at every boot: RMS, peak-to-peak and crest factor of the synthetic sine were within 5\% of their analytical values. All five fusion self-tests passed. In a 150\,s recorded session (Figure~\ref{fig:recorded-session}) all 149 fused windows combined both sensors with a skew below 1\,ms (the log holds 222 fused lines, because a window can be printed by either sensor's task or both; each window is counted once). The planned disturbance, between 50 and 70\,s on audible cues, did not happen because the cues were not heard; the board was disturbed from about 75 to 128\,s instead, and the log is annotated to say so. While the board was still (the first 70\,s), the fused vibration level $\sigma$ had a median of 0.041\,m/s\textsuperscript{2} and a maximum of 0.067\,m/s\textsuperscript{2}; during the hand disturbance it rose to 9.35\,m/s\textsuperscript{2}, and the largest peak-to-peak amplitude in a window was 77.6\,m/s\textsuperscript{2}, within 1\,m/s\textsuperscript{2} of the $\pm 8\,g$ range, so that peak was probably clipped. The disagreement figure $D$ ranged from 0.00 to 1.68. In an earlier disturbance test both sensors rose together (peak-to-peak from about 0.2 to 18.4 and 10.2\,m/s\textsuperscript{2}, crest factor from about 1.01 to 2.17 and 1.43, jerk from about 8 to 160 and 162\,m/s\textsuperscript{3}) and decayed together, so the calibrated pair also tracked each other under motion, not only at rest. R3 is met. These results show that the features are computed and combined as designed; they do not show how the features respond to the road or the motor.

\section{Position (R4)}

With the corrected parser, the receiver reached a three-dimensional fix 105\,s after a cold start (51\,s in an earlier session), with 23 to 25 satellites and a horizontal dilution of precision of about 0.9 to 1.5. Over 104 fixes the position was a median of 2.7\,m from a phone's position, 95\% within 11.0\,m and at most 12.3\,m away. The phone has an error of a few metres itself, so this is a consistency check, not an absolute accuracy; against the criterion of 10\,m, the median and most fixes pass but the 95th percentile does not. The speed field read 0 to 5.8 while stationary, so its units and low-speed noise are unconfirmed. The network position (v0.7.3) answered in about 0.7\,s with a reported accuracy of 550\,m.

\outstanding{time to first fix with assisted GNSS, and the speed field against a known speed on the vehicle.}

\section{Battery telemetry (R5)}

After the checksum correction the unit stored 14 consecutive valid records on the vehicle over about 140\,s: 74.78\,V, 0.00\,A and 60\% state of charge (3.12\,V per cell for 24 cells), at $-88$ to $-93$\,dBm, after which the connection was lost. R5 is partly met: valid frames were received, but the connection is not sustained and the voltage has not been compared with a meter.

\outstanding{sustained BMS session on the vehicle with the voltage checked against a meter.}

\section{Hazard sensing (R6 to R8)}

Table~\ref{tab:res-camera} summarises the hazard-sensing results and Table~\ref{tab:camera-latency} gives the latency breakdown. With four inference threads the detector ran at 19.5\,frames/s with a median of 102\,ms from sensor readout to result. The largest share, about 52\,ms, was the time from readout to the detector receiving the frame, which covers readout and the camera's image processing; inference took 36\,ms. With one thread inference took 126\,ms and the rate fell to 7.0\,frames/s, as in the first live runs. The processor reached 65.7\,\textdegree C without throttling. At 30\,km/h, 102\,ms is 0.85\,m of travel; the three-frame onset adds about two frame intervals, so an alert is raised about 0.2\,s, or 1.7\,m, after an object first appears. A person was detected with confidence 0.50 to 0.73; only the person class has been confirmed on live frames. These are bench figures with the camera indoors and no objects to track; the number of detections does not change the inference time, but the vehicle's heat and supply may, and detection has not been tested outdoors or in daylight, rain or glare. R6 and R7 are met on the bench.

\section{Telemetry, logging and recovery (R9, R10)}

The unit connected to the broker at the first attempt and published 47 times with no failures; a requested reconnection completed in 0.4\,s. With the modem's radio switched off for 60\,s, the firmware detected the loss from the modem's report 0.8\,s after the radio went off, or after three failed publishes at 20.7\,s, and retried after 30 and then 60\,s. In v0.5.0 it reconnected 36\,s after the radio returned, waiting for its next retry although data were back within 2.4\,s; v0.5.3 reconnected about 5\,s after. Logging continued throughout. A full read-back recovered all 6,020 stored records (2.2\,MB, about 16.7 hours at one record per 10\,s) after four passes; 5,545 were not valid JSON because of \texttt{nan}, and after the v0.6.3 substitution all 6,020 parse. R9 is met on the bench for the path to a test broker.

The dashboard line (v0.7.0) delivered 40 lines in 40\,s with no gap in the sequence numbers, every line valid JSON and at most 272 bytes, with both IMUs at 200\,Hz and no drops. Through the bridge on the Raspberry Pi~4 the record reached a laptop 0.37\,s old with no malformed lines, and opening the port did not reset the telemetry unit; the pre-test check on 4 October found the record 0.5\,s old and the telemetry answering in 18\,ms. R10 is met on the bench.

\outstanding{database synchronisation: completeness, delay, duplicates and backlog replay after an induced outage (firmware v0.7.4), and the same measures on the moving vehicle.}

\section{Driver display (R11, R12)}

Run on a laptop inside a copy of the dashboard's code, against the live detector on the Raspberry Pi~4 (1 October), the camera page showed video 0.6\,s after it was opened and decoded 14.2\,frames/s against the detector's 14.8, with no gap longer than 0.22\,s; with a person in front of the camera it listed a red ``Person, Immediate'' alert 0.5\,s after opening, and it switched to its offline state within a second of the detector stopping; the person stayed in view, so clearing on the page was seen only in the offline test and when the camera stopped, although the detector's log shows person alerts both raised and cleared. In live-only mode, run offscreen on a laptop against a simulated bridge (3 October), every page showed ``-{}-'' or ``Unavailable'' with the bridge down and never a simulated value, and the dashboard returned to the no-data state, not to simulation, when the bridge stopped. In an offscreen run of the whole integrated dashboard on the Raspberry Pi~5 against the live bridge (6 October), the Sensors page showed the ahead sensor at 0.43\,m in red, the right at 0.69\,m in amber and the left with no reading, and the banner appeared over the driving page; the page's use by touch had not been checked at the time. Reading the touch controller during 25 taps, the panel reported contact together with the first position in every tap, so it adds no measurable delay; the taps lasted 200 to 640\,ms and the buttons act on release, and the processor was idle (load average 0.04). R11 is met on the bench. R12, the glance demand, has not been measured, and the layout has not been checked against the legibility figures of Section~\ref{sec:lit-display}.

\outstanding{alert onset to screen on the Raspberry Pi~5, legibility in daylight, and glance observation on the vehicle.}

\section{Integration and power}

All telemetry-unit subsystems ran together on the target board, and the chain from the telemetry unit through the Raspberry Pi~4 to the dashboard on the Raspberry Pi~5 ran on the bench. The power-flag log found the cause of the under-voltage seen throughout the bench work: under-voltage began 14\,s after the telemetry unit was plugged into the Raspberry Pi~4's USB port, when its modem started, throttling the processor to 600\,MHz and slowing the detector from 14.3 to 12\,frames/s; on 4 October, under-voltage had held the detector at 6.1\,frames/s. The Raspberry Pi~4 reached 81\,\textdegree C without a fan and 48\,\textdegree C with one (Appendix~\ref{app:wiring}).

\outstanding{full-system test on the vehicle over a defined route, with the telemetry unit on its own supply.}
