\chapter{Results}
\label{chap:results}

This chapter gives the measured results against the requirements of Table~\ref{tab:requirements-mapping}. Bench results were measured on the target board and the two Raspberry Pis between 25 September and 15 October 2026; results from the vehicle (19 to 21 October) are marked as such in the text and in Table~\ref{tab:results-summary}. The raw evidence is indexed in Appendix~\ref{app:evidence}, and results still to be obtained are marked as outstanding where they will go.

The results come from the experiments of Section~\ref{sec:experiments}, collected and analysed as described in Sections~\ref{sec:data-collection} and~\ref{sec:data-analysis}, on the design of Chapter~\ref{chap:design}. The chapter is structured by requirement: acquisition, calibration and ride features (R1 to R3, Sections~\ref{sec:dual-imu-architecture} and~\ref{sec:ride-characterisation}), position (R4), battery telemetry (R5), hazard sensing (R6 to R8, Section~\ref{sec:camera-subsystem}), telemetry and recovery (R9, R10, Section~\ref{sec:mqtt-networking}) and the driver display (R11, R12, Section~\ref{sec:dashboard-page}). Within each section the earlier results come first and those of the first road session, where there are any, last. Integration and power follow, and Section~\ref{sec:res-summary} closes with a summary table against every requirement.

\section{Inertial acquisition (R1)}\label{sec:res-acq}

Table~\ref{tab:res-acquisition} summarises the acquisition results. Both IMUs held 200\,Hz with no dropped samples in every bench session, including with all subsystems running together on the target board. The only timing fault was the pause at each flash write. With a write every 10\,s (v0.5.0) both IMUs showed a 33 to 40\,ms gap at every write, twelve in two minutes; with logging switched off for the next two minutes the worst gap was 5.9\,ms. With buffered logging (v0.6.0), in a 330\,s run with five flushes, all ten gaps over 10\,ms (37 to 40\,ms, both IMUs at each flush) fell in the first statistics window reported after a flush, 0.2 to 1.05\,s after it (Figure~\ref{fig:res-imu-gaps}), and the worst gap elsewhere was again 5.9\,ms. Each flush occupied the modem task for 71 to 202\,ms, which does not affect acquisition. With v0.7.4 and its 94\,Hz filter the rate and drop count were unchanged (T15). ``No dropped samples'' does not mean even spacing: in the 25 September session the shortest interval fell to 0.8 to 1.0\,ms in each window with a write gap, as \texttt{vTaskDelayUntil} caught up, and jerk, computed with a fixed $\Delta t$, is distorted there. R1 is met on the bench as defined. The drop counter counts samples lost because the queue was full; a read that fails on the bus is not detected and would pass as a sample, so the loss figures cover the processing path, not the bus.

On the first road session (19 October; Section~\ref{sec:field-test-design}), which included about 16 minutes of driving, the drop count stayed at zero on both IMUs throughout. The unit sends one record a second, but the recorder polled twice a second and the bridge repeated its last record until a new line arrived, so the 10,287 rear replies hold 3,464 distinct records. Over the 5,557\,s from the first to the last (17:16:37 to 18:49:13), 2,093 seconds (38\,\%) have no record, and in the driving period (18:15 to 18:49) 67\,\% of seconds have one. The missing seconds are lines lost between the unit and the Raspberry Pi~4 over USB, the loss already seen on the bench (R10 below), and 27 of the records that arrived had damaged fields. Of the 3,437 readable records, 3,431 show both IMUs at 200.0 to 200.5\,Hz; five, at 18:03 to 18:05, show 197 to 204.5\,Hz, an uneven split of samples between two-second windows; and one, at 18:29:16 while driving, shows IMU1 with 4 samples in a 2\,s window instead of 400, a stall of nearly 2\,s that the drop count does not show and whose cause is not known. On the vehicle R1 is therefore partly met: no samples were dropped and the rate held except for that stall, but a third of the per-second records did not reach the recorder.

On 21 October, with the unit on its own battery and its record sent over the new wired link (Section~\ref{sec:inv-power}), two faults appeared on the bench. After about 23 minutes both IMUs' drop counts were rising, by about 167 and 46 a second, until the unit was reset; after another reset IMU2 was not detected until the next. Neither cause has been found, and both have to be cleared before the second road session.

\section{Calibration (R2)}

Before calibration, the two IMUs on the development board read about 5.7\% above and 2.6\% below standard gravity at rest, about 8\% apart. The factors stored at their first calibration were 0.9773 and 0.9901, after which both read 9.80 to 9.81\,m/s\textsuperscript{2}, agreeing within measurement noise. The factors imply mean magnitudes at the moment of calibration of 10.03 and 9.90\,m/s\textsuperscript{2}, not the readings seen earlier that day, so the uncalibrated readings had shifted between sessions; the likely cause, not confirmed, is that the sensors were moved in between. On the target board the first boot measured 9.44 and 9.57\,m/s\textsuperscript{2} (factors 1.0390 and 1.0245), and with those factors a later session read median resting magnitudes of 9.93 and 10.10\,m/s\textsuperscript{2}, 1.3\% and 2.9\% above gravity. R2 was met immediately after calibration but not in the later session, which confirms that a single factor holds only for the orientation in which it was measured.

\section{Ride features and fusion (R3)}

The feature self-test passed at every boot: RMS, peak-to-peak and crest factor of the synthetic sine were within 5\% of their analytical values. All five fusion self-tests passed. In a 150\,s recorded session (Figure~\ref{fig:recorded-session}) all 149 fused windows combined both sensors with a skew below 1\,ms (the log holds 222 fused lines, because a window can be printed by either sensor's task or both; each window is counted once). The planned disturbance, between 50 and 70\,s on audible cues, did not happen because the cues were not heard; the board was disturbed from about 75 to 128\,s instead, and the log is annotated to say so. While the board was still (the first 70\,s), the fused vibration level $\sigma$ had a median of 0.041\,m/s\textsuperscript{2} and a maximum of 0.067\,m/s\textsuperscript{2}; during the hand disturbance it rose to 9.35\,m/s\textsuperscript{2}, and the largest peak-to-peak amplitude in a window was 77.6\,m/s\textsuperscript{2}, within 1\,m/s\textsuperscript{2} of the $\pm 8\,g$ range, so that peak was probably clipped. The disagreement figure $D$ ranged from 0.00 to 1.68. In an earlier disturbance test both sensors rose together (peak-to-peak from about 0.2 to 18.4 and 10.2\,m/s\textsuperscript{2}, crest factor from about 1.01 to 2.17 and 1.43, jerk from about 8 to 160 and 162\,m/s\textsuperscript{3}) and decayed together, so the calibrated pair also tracked each other under motion, not only at rest. R3 is met. These results show that the features are computed and combined as designed; they do not show how the features respond to the road or the motor.

\section{Position (R4)}

With the corrected parser, the receiver reached a three-dimensional fix 105\,s after a cold start (51\,s in an earlier session), with 23 to 25 satellites and a horizontal dilution of precision of about 0.9 to 1.5. Over 104 fixes the position was a median of 2.7\,m from a phone's position, 95\% within 11.0\,m and at most 12.3\,m away. The phone has an error of a few metres itself, so this is a consistency check, not an absolute accuracy; against the criterion of 10\,m, the median and most fixes pass but the 95th percentile does not. The speed field read 0 to 5.8 while stationary; the manual gives knots, but the readings and their low-speed noise have not been checked against a known speed. The network position (v0.7.3) answered in about 0.7\,s with a reported accuracy of 550\,m.

On the road session of 19 October the receiver reported no fix in any of the 3,464 records, so no position or speed was recorded during the drive. The cause was that no GNSS antenna had been connected to the board, not a fault in the receiver; only the network-based position (about 550\,m accuracy) was available. R4 is therefore untested on the vehicle rather than failed.

\outstanding{time to first fix with assisted GNSS, and the speed field against a known speed on the vehicle.}

\section{Battery telemetry (R5)}

After the checksum correction the unit stored 14 consecutive valid records on the vehicle over about 140\,s: 74.78\,V, 0.00\,A and 60\% state of charge (3.12\,V per cell for 24 cells), at $-88$ to $-93$\,dBm, after which the connection was lost. On 16 October, with the telemetry unit moved to within about 2\,m of the pack (Section~\ref{sec:two-sided}), the link held: 60\,\% state of charge, 74.67 to 74.68\,V and 0.0\,A with the vehicle off, each record 0.2 to 1.3\,s old. Before that the laptop had found the unit advertising as ``DB24SA01L24S150ABU'' at $-100$\,dBm, at the edge of reception, which explains the earlier drop-outs. The name gives a 24-cell pack and a 150\,A BMS; 74.7\,V at 60\,\% (3.11\,V per cell) fits lithium iron phosphate, about 60 to 88\,V over its range. With the system mounted on the vehicle on 19 October the link held throughout the session, at 60\,\% and 74.57 to 74.58\,V, with each record under 3\,s old. Until the road session the current had not been seen under load.

On the road session the link held throughout. The current reached 46.9\,A under load, the pack voltage fell from 74.6 to 72.8\,V while driving, and the state of charge from 60 to 58\,\%. R5 is partly met: the link held on the vehicle under load, but the voltage has not been compared with a meter.

\section{Hazard sensing (R6 to R8)}

Table~\ref{tab:res-camera} summarises the hazard-sensing results and Table~\ref{tab:camera-latency} gives the latency breakdown. With four inference threads and nothing else running (no bridge, no fusion polling, live view not watched; 30 September) the detector ran at 19.5\,frames/s with a median of 102\,ms from sensor readout to result; about 52\,ms of that was readout and the camera's image processing, and 36\,ms inference. With the whole system running on a healthy Raspberry Pi~4 (11 October, from the service's own timing lines) it ran at 14.6 to 14.7\,frames/s, median 118 to 122\,ms, 95th percentile 146 to 148\,ms; the cause of the difference has not been isolated, and these are the figures that represent the rear unit on the vehicle; the front unit on the Raspberry Pi~5 is in Table~\ref{tab:res-camera} and Section~\ref{sec:two-sided}. The three-frame onset adds about two frame intervals, so at the detector an alert is raised about 0.26\,s, or 2.2\,m at 30\,km/h, after an object first appears; the bridge's and dashboard's polling and rendering add an unmeasured delay before the driver sees it. A person was detected with confidence 0.50 to 0.73; only the person class has been confirmed on live frames. These are bench figures with the camera indoors and no objects to track; the number of detections does not change the inference time, but the vehicle's heat and supply may, and detection has not been tested outdoors or in daylight, rain or glare. R7 is met at the detector on the bench, for the person class. R6 is met for the pulse itself, which the camera unit timestamps with a spread of under 0.7\,ms. The offset between the camera's and the telemetry unit's clocks, which aligning the two streams needs, has not been measured; the median interval of 500.06\,ms also means the two clocks run about 120 parts per million apart, about 0.4\,s an hour, so the alignment must fit a drift as well as an offset. The front camera on the Raspberry Pi~5 has no pulse wire.

\section{Telemetry, logging and recovery (R9, R10)}

The unit connected to the broker at the first attempt and published 47 times with no failures; a requested reconnection completed in 0.4\,s. With the modem's radio switched off for 60\,s, the firmware detected the loss from the modem's report 0.8\,s after the radio went off, or after three failed publishes at 20.7\,s, and retried after 30 and then 60\,s. In v0.5.0 it reconnected 36\,s after the radio returned, waiting for its next retry although data were back within 2.4\,s; v0.5.3 reconnected about 5\,s after. Logging continued throughout. A full read-back recovered all 6,020 stored records (2.2\,MB, about 16.7 hours at one record per 10\,s) after four passes; 5,545 were not valid JSON because of \texttt{nan}, and after the v0.6.3 substitution all 6,020 parse.

Synchronisation with the database was tested with v0.7.4 (test T15, 11 October, indoors, public test broker; Table~\ref{tab:res-sync}). Every record was stored once, but 24 only after a backfill from the unit's log: two published while the ingest was restarted, the rest before an ingest fault found during the test was fixed (its persistent session on the broker stopped delivering while still connected; such sessions are now opt-in). The public broker acknowledges a record with no subscriber present and does not queue it, so on this broker reading the log back is the safety net. R9 is met on the bench, including the database.

In a longer bench run (12 to 13 October, 4.7 hours, v0.7.4 with the modem and the database sync active) the bridge counted 858 unusable dashboard lines among 9,229 lines read (9.3\,\%); the cause has not been found. A skipped line delays the dashboard by a second; the unit's own log and the uplink are not affected. On the road session a third of the per-second records did not reach the recorder for the same reason (Section~\ref{sec:res-acq}). Since 21 October the record travels over a wired UART instead of USB (Section~\ref{sec:inv-power}); none of the first 2,489 lines was bad, but a long run has not been done. R10 is met only in the 40-line test and is partly met overall.

\section{Driver display (R11, R12)}

Run on a laptop inside a copy of the dashboard's code, against the live detector on the Raspberry Pi~4 (1 October), the camera page showed video 0.6\,s after it was opened and decoded 14.2\,frames/s against the detector's 14.8, with no gap longer than 0.22\,s; with a person in front of the camera it listed a red ``Person, Immediate'' alert 0.5\,s after the page was opened with the person already in view (not an onset latency), and it switched to its offline state within a second of the detector stopping; the person stayed in view, so clearing on the page was seen only in the offline test and when the camera stopped, although the detector's log shows person alerts both raised and cleared. In live-only mode, run offscreen on a laptop against a simulated bridge (3 October), every page showed ``-{}-'' or ``Unavailable'' with the bridge down and never a simulated value, and the dashboard returned to the no-data state, not to simulation, when the bridge stopped. In an offscreen run of the whole integrated dashboard on the Raspberry Pi~5 against the live bridge (6 October), the Sensors page showed the ahead sensor at 0.43\,m in red, the right at 0.69\,m in amber and the left with no reading, and the banner appeared over the driving page; the page's use by touch had not been checked at the time. Reading the touch controller during 25 taps, the panel reported contact together with the first position in every tap, so it adds no measurable delay; the taps lasted 200 to 640\,ms and the buttons act on release, and the processor was idle (load average 0.04). R11 is met on the bench, from laptop and offscreen runs. R12, the glance demand, has not been measured, and the layout has not been checked against the legibility figures of Section~\ref{sec:lit-display}.

\section{Integration and power}

All telemetry-unit subsystems ran together on the target board, and the chain from the telemetry unit through the Raspberry Pi~4 to the dashboard on the Raspberry Pi~5 ran on the bench. The under-voltage that throttled the Raspberry Pi~4 through the bench work, and held the detector at 6.1\,frames/s on 4 October, was traced to the telemetry unit's modem on its USB port (Section~\ref{sec:inv-power}). The Pi reached 81\,\textdegree C without a fan and 48 to 66\,\textdegree C with one.

On 19 October the whole system was mounted on the vehicle and checked in the lab. Both cameras and detectors ran (front 17.5 to 22.0\,frames/s, 53 to 102\,ms; rear 14.4 to 14.5\,frames/s, 126 to 151\,ms, slower while the Raspberry Pi~4 was throttled). Both time-of-flight boards answered, both IMUs held 200\,Hz with no drops, and the battery link reported on the vehicle. The Ethernet link ran at 1000\,Mb/s with a 0.25\,ms round trip, and the recorder on the Raspberry Pi~5 received all four sources at 2\,Hz. The Raspberry Pi~4 logged 11 to 16 under-voltage warnings per boot with the telemetry unit still on its USB port, and both computers restarted during the mounting work (Section~\ref{sec:inv-power}). Table~\ref{tab:power-budget} gives the power budget against the 72\,V pack (GA~5). Only the Raspberry Pi~5 figure is measured, from its own power-management readings on 14 October with the dashboard running (2.2 to 3.0\,W on its internal rails, before it took on the front camera, detector and time-of-flight board on 15 October, so its share in the final layout is higher than shown); the rest are estimates from datasheets and published bench figures, to be replaced by inline USB meter readings. On the same day the Raspberry Pi~4, with the detector using three of its four cores, reported that its supply had fallen below the under-voltage threshold since boot (\texttt{get\_throttled} 0x50000), so its present supply is not adequate.

At about 20\,W the added electronics use 20\,Wh per hour, 160\,Wh over an eight-hour shift. Against a 7.2\,kWh pack, the figure the team's dashboard uses (not confirmed; the BMS's name gives 24 cells but not the capacity), that is 0.28\,\% of the pack per hour and 2.2\,\% per shift, and about 0.8\,\% of the motor's rated input of 2.5\,kW. The draw is small while driving but not when parked: left on, the full system would empty the same pack in about 15 days, so it must switch off with the vehicle, leaving at most the telemetry unit (about 1\,W) on. The converter must accept the pack's whole voltage range, about 60 to 88\,V for its 24 lithium iron phosphate cells (R5 above), and be rated to at least 100\,V for transients, and deliver at least 40\,W at 5\,V for the peaks, with a fuse of about 2\,A on its 72\,V input.

On the road session the recorder on the Raspberry Pi~5 ran for 108.6 minutes without the laptop and did not restart while driving, and the front sources were recorded throughout. The rear unit answered it until 18:49:13, just after the last driving segment, and not for the last 15 minutes (1,785 failed requests), although the rear detector kept logging its alerts until 19:02; the cause was not recorded. Before that the rear data had one gap, of 409\,s, before the drive, and a third of its seconds had no new record (Section~\ref{sec:res-acq}).
