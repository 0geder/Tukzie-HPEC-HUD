\chapter{Results}
\label{chap:results}

This chapter collects the measured results by research question. Every figure below was measured on the bench, on the telemetry unit's target board and the two Raspberry Pis, between 25 September and 6 October 2026; the conditions and methods are given in the Methodology sections referred to, and the raw evidence is indexed in Appendix~\ref{app:evidence}. No measurement has yet been made on the moving vehicle, and each section says which results depend on that. Table~\ref{tab:results-summary} at the end of the chapter returns to the requirements of Table~\ref{tab:requirements-mapping}.

\section{Inertial acquisition integrity (RQ3)}

Table~\ref{tab:res-acquisition} summarises the acquisition results. Both inertial sensors held their 200\,Hz rate with no dropped samples in every recorded session, including with all subsystems running together on the target board (Sections~\ref{sec:dual-imu-architecture} and~\ref{sec:full-system-integration}). The only timing fault found was a pause of 33 to 40\,ms whenever the log was written to flash. Writing the log in one buffered append about once a minute reduced the number of such pauses from six a minute to one, without shortening each pause (Section~\ref{sec:local-logging}). Each pause appears as a gap of that length in the sample timestamps; the per-second sample counts and the drop counters were unaffected.

\section{Sensor calibration}

At rest, before calibration, IMU1 read about 5.7\% above true gravity and IMU2 about 2.6\% below it, a disagreement of about 8\% between two nominally identical sensors, within what the accelerometer's datasheet permits (Section~\ref{sec:imu-magnitude-calibration}). The firmware therefore stores a scale factor for each sensor, measured at its first boot on each board: 0.9773 and 0.9901 on the development board, and 1.0390 and 1.0245 on the target board, where the resting magnitudes were 9.44 and 9.57\,m/s\textsuperscript{2}. The factors differ between boards for the same sensors, which shows that calibration has to be repeated in the final mounted position. How closely the calibrated sensors agree under the vehicle's own vibration has not yet been measured.

\section{Ride characterisation (RQ1, RQ4)}

The feature computation was checked against a synthetic 5\,Hz signal of 200 samples: root-mean-square, peak-to-peak amplitude and crest factor were all within 5\% of their analytical values (Section~\ref{sec:ride-characterisation}). The dual-IMU fusion passed all five of its start-up self-tests, and in a 150\,s recorded bench session all 149 fused windows combined both sensors, with a skew between them below 1\,ms (Section~\ref{sec:imu-fusion}). During the hand-disturbed part of that session the largest peak-to-peak amplitude in a window was 77.6\,m/s\textsuperscript{2}, inside the $\pm8g$ range set for the sensors.

These results show that the features are computed correctly and that the two sensors are combined as designed. They do not show how the features respond to road surfaces or to the motor, because no data have yet been recorded on the vehicle; the speed-normalised index has also not yet been computed against a real speed, since the units of the GNSS speed field are unconfirmed (Section~\ref{sec:speed-norm}). RQ1 and RQ4 are therefore not yet answered.

\section{Battery-management telemetry}

On the vehicle on 30 September 2026, after the frame checksum was corrected, the unit stored 14 valid battery records over about 140\,s: 74.78\,V, 0.00\,A and 60\% state of charge, with a received signal of $-88$ to $-93$\,dBm, after which the connection was lost (Section~\ref{sec:bms-interface}). The reading is plausible for a 24-cell pack at rest (3.12\,V per cell), but it has not been compared with the pack's own app, and the connection is not yet reliable over a sustained session, so a discharge curve has not been measured.

\section{Position}

Outdoors, with the firmware that parses the modem's response correctly, the receiver reached a three-dimensional fix 105\,s after a cold start (51\,s in an earlier session), with 23 to 25 satellites and a horizontal dilution of precision of about 0.9 to 1.5. Over 104 fixes the position was a median of 2.7\,m from a phone's position, 95\% within 11.0\,m and at most 12.3\,m away (Section~\ref{sec:gnss-outdoor}). Because the phone's own position has an error of a few metres, this is a consistency check rather than an absolute accuracy. The speed field read between 0 and 5.8 while the board was stationary, so its units and its noise at low speed are still to be confirmed before it can be used.

\section{Cellular synchronisation and data integrity (RQ3)}

On the bench the unit connected to the broker at the first attempt and published 47 times with no failures; a requested reconnection completed in 0.4\,s (Section~\ref{sec:mqtt-networking}). With the modem's radio switched off for 60\,s to simulate a loss of coverage, firmware v0.5.0 detected the loss and reconnected 36\,s after the radio returned, because it waited for its next scheduled retry; v0.5.3 watches for the modem's own report that data is available again and reconnected about 5\,s after the radio returned. Logging to flash continued throughout, independent of the connection. A full read-back of the log recovered all 6,020 records (2.2\,MB, about 16.7 hours of operation at one record per 10\,s), and exposed a fault: 5,545 records were not valid JSON because missing values were written as \texttt{nan}; since v0.6.3 they are written as \texttt{null}, and with that substitution all 6,020 records parse (Section~\ref{sec:local-logging}). The broker is a public test broker, and no central database receives the data yet.

\section{Driver display (RQ2)}

Against the live detector, the dashboard's camera page showed video 0.6\,s after it was opened and decoded 14.2 frames per second against the detector's 14.8, with no gap longer than 0.22\,s; with a person in front of the camera it listed a red ``Person, Immediate'' alert 0.5\,s after opening, and it switched to an offline state within a second of the detector stopping (Section~\ref{sec:dashboard-page}). In live-only mode the dashboard shows only real data, with ``--'' for anything missing, where it had previously shown simulated values without any sign (Section~\ref{sec:live-only}). Since 6 October the dashboard has started as the default on the Raspberry Pi~5 at boot, and it shows the time-of-flight distances and the fused hazards on a Sensors page, with a banner over every page for an immediate hazard (Section~\ref{sec:pi5-default}). Reading the touch panel directly over 25 taps, the panel reported contact together with the first position, so it adds no measurable delay; the taps lasted 200 to 640\,ms, and the buttons act on release. The time a driver needs to read the display, the quantity that RQ2 is ultimately about, has not been measured, and the layout has not yet been checked against the published glance and legibility figures (Chapter~\ref{chap:lit}).

\section{Full-system operation and power}

All subsystems of the telemetry unit have run together on the target board, and the whole chain from the telemetry unit through the Raspberry Pi~4 to the dashboard on the Raspberry Pi~5 has run on the bench. A 20-minute log of the Raspberry Pi~4's power flags on 6 October found the cause of the under-voltage seen throughout the bench work: the flags were clear with nothing attached, and under-voltage began 14\,s after the telemetry unit was plugged into the Raspberry Pi's USB port, when its LTE modem started, slowing the detector from 14.3 to 12 frames per second (Appendix~\ref{app:evidence}). A full-system test on the vehicle over a defined route has not yet been carried out.
