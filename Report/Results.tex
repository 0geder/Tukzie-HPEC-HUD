\chapter{Results}
\label{chap:results}

This chapter gives the measured results of the experiments of Section~\ref{sec:experiments}, analysed as in Section~\ref{sec:data-analysis}, against the requirements of Table~\ref{tab:requirements-mapping}. Bench results were measured between 25 September and 22 October 2026; results from the vehicle (the first road session, 19 October) are marked as such. It is structured by requirement, earlier results first and the road session last, and closes with a summary table (Section~\ref{sec:res-summary}). The headline result of each test is given here; the full run histories, per-run breakdowns and longer tables are in Appendix~\ref{app:results}, and the raw evidence is indexed in Appendix~\ref{app:evidence}. Results that the second road session will supply are marked as outstanding.

\section{Inertial acquisition (R1)}
\label{sec:res-acq}

Both IMUs held 200.0 to 200.5\,Hz with no dropped samples in the bench sessions of Table~\ref{tab:res-acquisition}, including with all subsystems running together and with the 94\,Hz filter of v0.7.4 The only timing fault was the pause at each flash write. Figure~\ref{fig:res-imu-gaps} shows the longest interval in each 2\,s window: writing every 10\,s (v0.5.0), 30 of 151 windows per IMU had a gap of 33 to 40\,ms; with buffered writes (v0.6.0) only the 5 windows after the five flushes did, and the worst gap elsewhere was 5.9\,ms in both runs. No dropped samples does not mean even spacing: after a pause the missed samples are read about 1\,ms apart, which distorts the jerk of that window. The two inertial-only nodes of the second session, which keep no flash log, removed the pause altogether: on the vehicle on 27 October each held 200.00\,Hz with no interval longer than 5.2\,ms in about 35,000 samples, and at rest they read 9.845 (rear) and 9.941\,m/s\textsuperscript{2} (front) against 9.81, within 0.4 and 1.3\,\%, with both Raspberry Pis' clocks in step. R1 is met on the bench as defined; up to v0.7.4.1 a read that failed on the bus was not detected, so those figures cover the processing path, not the bus.

On the first road session, with about 16 minutes of driving, the drop count stayed at zero on both IMUs, and 3,431 of the 3,437 readable records show both at 200.0 to 200.5\,Hz; one record, while driving, shows a stall of IMU1 of nearly 2\,s whose cause is not known. Of the 5,557 seconds from the first to the last record, 2,093 (38\,\%) have none at the recorder, most likely lost on the USB link (R10 below). On the vehicle R1 is not met: the stall lost about 396 samples that the drop count does not show.

On 21 and 22 October, on its own battery and the wired link, the rear unit's drop counts rose until a reset: the statistics and ride tasks had stopped on the IMU core, and v0.7.6 moved them (Section~\ref{sec:inv-stall}). On v0.7.6 both nodes ran at 200\,Hz with no drops or read failures, and a 62-minute soak of the rear unit under the fault's conditions had no restarts, drops, read failures or late slots, with the rate outside 199.5 to 200.5\,Hz in 3 of 726 polls (Table~\ref{tab:res-twonode}). The soak shows that v0.7.6 ran through the fault's conditions, not that the trigger was present. With v0.7.8 the unit re-initialised IMU1 by itself after each of four pulls of its supply wire, with no drops and no reset (Section~\ref{sec:impl-health}). R1 is met on both nodes on the bench.

On the road session of 27 October (11:30 to 12:40, laps at 10, 15 and 20\,km/h), the two inertial-only nodes held 200.005 and 200.006\,Hz: the front node delivered 1,791,190 samples with no sequence gap and the rear 1,184,780 with 910 missing in transport (0.08\,\%), with no read failures, raw drops or clipped samples, one stuck value and four lines rejected by their checksum. R1 is met on the vehicle for rate and spacing; the 910 samples lost in transport break its zero-loss clause at the rear, although the node itself dropped none.

\section{Calibration (R2)}
\label{sec:res-cal}

Before calibration the two IMUs read about 5.7\,\% above and 2.6\,\% below standard gravity at rest; after it both read 9.80 to 9.81\,m/s\textsuperscript{2}. On the target board a later session read 1.3\,\% and 2.9\,\% above gravity with the same factors, which suggests that a single factor holds only in the orientation in which it was measured. On the front node a recalibration on 22 October repeated the first factor within 0.01\,\% (1.0036 and 1.0035), with the board not yet mounted (Section~\ref{sec:resx-cal}).

On 23 October both nodes were recalibrated in their mounted positions, with the vehicle on stands, the motor off and the wheel still (markers R2 RECAL). The front node read 9.886\,m/s\textsuperscript{2} at rest and stored a factor of 0.9919, against 1.0035 on the bench: the same sensor needs a factor 1.2\,\% different once mounted. The rear node reports its result only on its unconnected USB console, so its factor was computed from the raw stream of the 12.9\,s after its restart by the same method (2,580 samples, none missing): 0.9907\,g, or 9.715\,m/s\textsuperscript{2}, giving 1.0094, with a noise standard deviation of 0.003\,g; its pod sits within 1.5$^\circ$ of level. These are the factors' own definition, not an independent check: with the earlier bench factors the parked vehicle read 10.04 and 10.34\,m/s\textsuperscript{2} on 19 October (Table~\ref{tab:res-flashlog}), and whether the mounted factors hold is still to be checked.

\section{Ride features and fusion (R3)}
\label{sec:res-ride}

The feature self-test and all five fusion self-tests passed at every boot, and in a 150\,s bench session all 149 fused windows combined both sensors with a skew below 1\,ms; under a hand disturbance both sensors rose and decayed together (Section~\ref{sec:resx-ride}). R3 is met: the features are computed and combined as designed, which does not show how they respond to the road or the motor.

\subsection{Run-1 flash log}
\label{sec:res-flashlog}

The unit's own log of the session, read back on 22 October, holds the per-IMU features of one 1\,s window about every 23\,s (1,154 records; Figure~\ref{fig:res-flashlog} and Table~\ref{tab:res-flashlog} in Appendix~\ref{app:results}). In the 26 records identified as driving by current, $\sigma$ rose from about 0.03 to a median of about 2\,m/s\textsuperscript{2}, peak-to-peak from about 0.17 to 11 to 14\,m/s\textsuperscript{2}, crest factor from 1.01 to about 1.6 and jerk from about 7 to 250\,m/s\textsuperscript{3}. The two sensors, both on the rear unit that day, tracked each other closely: the ratio of their $\sigma$ had a median of 1.06 and a correlation of 0.97, and $D$ a median of 0.074. This shows the size of the response and that two sensors measuring the same motion agreed, not its repeatability, its dependence on speed (there was no GNSS antenna) or how the front and rear compare.

\subsection{Stand runs}
\label{sec:res-stands}

The lab part of run 2 took place on 23 October with the drive wheel raised on stands; the road part was driven on 27 October. In two stand runs (15:53:11 to 15:55:50 and 16:08:12 to 16:10:20) the motor drew a mean of 3.2 and 4.5\,A (peaks 4.4 and 6.2\,A). In the raw 200\,Hz streams, which had no gaps, the dynamic RMS of the rear acceleration magnitude rose from 0.027\,m/s\textsuperscript{2} at rest to 0.13 and 0.20, five to seven times, with the energy between about 40 and 70\,Hz. The front node rose only from 0.020 to 0.026 and 0.035, but in the second run it showed the same lines as the rear, at 42.75, 49.0 and 64.0\,Hz, so powertrain vibration reaches the front through the frame (Figure~\ref{fig:res-stands}). The throttle settings and wheel speed were not recorded, so the lines cannot yet be assigned to motor orders.

The road part was driven on 27 October as laps with stops between them and no typed markers; the pass windows were derived afterwards from the phone's GNSS speed and the battery current (\texttt{BenchTest/\allowbreak{}analysis/\allowbreak{}auto\_markers.py}), so they include each lap's turns and run-up, and no stand runs, bump passes or video were recorded. On a smooth loop, five passes at 10\,km/h gave a rear $a_w$ of 1.78\,m/s\textsuperscript{2} with a CV of 5.0\,\% (VDV 2.4\,\%, $\sigma$ 4.6\,\%) and a front $a_w$ of 1.16\,m/s\textsuperscript{2} with a CV of 5.3\,\%, inside the 10\,\% criterion at both ends (n = 5). On a rougher loop about 200\,m away, identified by position and by the session's timing, four passes at the same speed read 3.61 (rear) and 2.26\,m/s\textsuperscript{2} (front), about twice the smooth values, with CVs near 20\,\% because the laps covered different stretches. Only two passes were driven at 15\,km/h (rear $a_w$ 2.43 and 2.39) and one at 20\,km/h (3.59), too few for their repeatability: the car park was too small to hold the higher speeds for long, and the light trike, without its body, became unstable in the corners (the phone recorded up to 28.3\,km/h). The ICC was not computed: without section lines the laps cannot be aligned bin by bin. On the smooth loop $I_v$ rose with speed (rear 1.14, 1.59 and 2.44 at about 10, 13 and 18\,km/h), so it is not speed-invariant over this range. In each pass the 23 October powertrain lines (42.75, 49.0 and 64.0\,Hz) held 1 to 3\,\% of the 0.5 to 80\,Hz energy, while 65 to 75\,\% lay between 4 and 8\,Hz; a coasting pass on the smooth loop gave a rear $a_w$ of 1.80\,m/s\textsuperscript{2} against 1.78 powered at the same speed. The front-to-rear delay was not estimated: the correlation stayed below 0.11 and the wheelbase was not measured.

\section{Position (R4)}
\label{sec:res-position}

The receiver reached a fix 105\,s after a cold start, and over 104 fixes the position was a median of 2.7\,m from a phone's, 95\,\% within 11.0\,m: against the 10\,m criterion the median passes but the 95th percentile does not, and the phone's own error makes this a consistency check. The speed field has not been checked against a known speed. On the road session no GNSS antenna was connected, so no position or speed was recorded; R4 is untested on the vehicle rather than failed (Section~\ref{sec:resx-gnss}).

With the antenna on the modem's GNSS connector (fitted 22 October), in more than 45 minutes in the lab on 23 October, with the assisted data loaded, the receiver reported no fix; the modem's network position was available throughout, at an estimated 550\,m accuracy, which is not usable on the road. With a view of the sky on 24 October, off the vehicle, it held a 3D fix for 10 minutes (179 fixes, 23 to 29 satellites, HDOP 0.77 to 1.1), within a median 1.8\,m and at most 5.9\,m of its median position, so the lab's failure was the sky view, not the receiver; standing still, 42 of the speed readings were not zero (up to 6.97, units unconfirmed), so the analysis ignores speeds below a minimum. As a speed and position reference that does not depend on the vehicle's receiver, a phone on the vehicle now streams its GNSS as NMEA over its hotspot to the Raspberry Pi~5, which logs every sentence with its own clock and passes the speed to the dashboard (Section~\ref{sec:impl-phone-gps}). In the lab it delivered 2,006 sentences with no checksum errors and a fix on 13 satellites, and with a simulated 20\,km/h drive the dashboard showed 20\,km/h; it has not yet been compared with a known speed.

On 27 October the phone gave the speed throughout the laps (14,181 fixes) until its log stopped at 12:29, and the vehicle receiver held a fix outdoors from 11:36. On the smooth loop its pass-mean speed, read as knots, was 0.9 to 1.4 times the phone's and its position 8 to 25\,m from the phone's, but its value changed only about every 25\,s, because the cellular retries starved its polls (Section~\ref{sec:investigations}); from 12:13 it held a stale position and zero speed while still flagged as fixed. The phone was therefore the speed source, and the trike's speedometer was hard to read in sunlight. No side video was taken. Read by eye during the laps, the dashboard showed about 1\,km/h less than the trike's own speedometer (for example 10 against 11\,km/h) and lagged it by a second or two when the speed changed, as the phone's once-a-second fixes and the dashboard's polling predict; since speedometers are made to read slightly high, the two agree, but the check was not recorded and R4 stays partly met.

\section{Battery telemetry (R5)}
\label{sec:res-battery}

Once the unit was moved near the pack (Section~\ref{sec:two-sided}), the BMS link held, and on the road session it held throughout: the current reached 46.9\,A under load (48.03\,A in the unit's own log), the voltage fell from 74.6 to 72.8\,V and the state of charge from 60 to 58\,\%. The pack name gives 24 cells, consistent with 74.7\,V; the chemistry is not confirmed. R5 is partly met: the voltage has not been compared with a meter (Section~\ref{sec:resx-bms}).

The BMS voltage was not compared with a meter on 27 October; it read 73.9 to 74.4\,V and 54 to 57\,\% over the session, consistent with the pack's own display, which was read by eye only. R5 stays partly met.

\label{sec:res-hazard}

\section{Driver display (R11, R12)}
\label{sec:res-display}

On a laptop against the live detector, the camera page showed video within 0.6\,s at 14.2\,frames/s and listed a person alert 0.5\,s after it was opened with the person in view. In live-only mode every page showed ``-{}-'' or ``Unavailable'' with the bridge down, never a simulated value. R11 is partly met: alerts were seen to clear only in offline runs. At the measured eye distance of 0.62\,m (four readings from the seat, 0.60 to 0.63\,m) from the 10.1-inch panel (0.170\,mm per pixel), the speed digits are 100\,px tall, 94 arc minutes, far above the 20 recommended by ISO~15008; the alert banner's capitals are 11\,px, 10 arc minutes, below even the 12 minimum, and only 4 of 110 labels reach the acceptable 16. The median touch target is 10.9\,mm, and 5 of 26 reach 17.5\,mm (Section~\ref{sec:resx-display}). The speed is legible at a glance; the warnings are not.

Display photographs in sunlight were not taken, so contrast in daylight remains unmeasured (Section~\ref{sec:disc-display}).

\section{Integration and power}
\label{sec:res-integration}

The chain from the telemetry unit through the Raspberry Pi~4 to the dashboard ran on the bench, and on 19 October the whole system was mounted on the vehicle: both detectors ran (front 17.5 to 22.0\,frames/s, 53 to 102\,ms; rear 14.4 to 14.5\,frames/s, 126 to 151\,ms), both time-of-flight boards answered, both IMUs held 200\,Hz and the Ethernet link ran at 1000\,Mb/s. The Raspberry Pi~4's under-voltage was traced to the modem on its USB port (Section~\ref{sec:inv-power}). On the road session the recorder ran for 108.6 minutes without restarting, though the rear unit stopped answering it for the last 15 minutes, cause not recorded. The estimated added load is about 20\,W from the 72\,V pack while driving, 0.28\,\% of a 7.2\,kWh pack an hour, but enough to empty it in about 15 days if left on, so the system must switch off with the vehicle (Table~\ref{tab:power-budget}, Section~\ref{sec:resx-power}). Only the Raspberry Pi~5's figure is measured.

On 23 October, in the lab, both nodes, both detectors (front 23.8, rear 14.3\,frames/s), the recorder and the screen capture ran with the Raspberry Pis joined by Ethernet, the Raspberry Pi~4's clock synchronised to the Raspberry Pi~5's (offset estimate under 1\,$\mu$s, error bound about $\pm 79\,\mu$s). The Raspberry Pi~4 restarted twice (about 14:17 and 16:22:48) and flagged under-voltage repeatedly (10 events in 44 minutes); the second restart came 40\,s after the rear unit restarted for its recalibration, the pattern of the bench reboots when the modem powered up from the Raspberry Pi~4's USB port. Its supply is therefore not yet adequate on the vehicle.

On 26 October the Raspberry Pi~4 stopped booting after a day of under-voltage flags and repeated restarts, and was replaced with another board on the same SD card. The new board reported no under-voltage with only its own loads (0x0 after 24 minutes) but flagged it again, in short repeated events, while the telemetry unit was also powered from its USB port, so the old board's supply path was at least part of the fault and the telemetry unit must not draw from the Raspberry Pi. On the same day the rear MPU6050 stopped answering on the telemetry unit (no device at 0x68, and with a weak pull-down neither data line showed the module's pull-up resistors, so the wiring or the module's supply was open; no short between the lines). The sensor itself worked on a second ESP32-S3 board, which became the rear inertial node: the front node's firmware named \texttt{rear}, at 200\,Hz with no drops, on the Raspberry Pi~4's USB and captured like the front node, while the telemetry unit kept the battery, GNSS and cellular link on the wired serial line. With that arrangement the readiness check passed on every critical item, and the recorder logged all six sources (24 of 24 polls in its first 12\,s).

On 27 October the Raspberry Pi~4, with the telemetry unit also powered from its USB port, flagged under-voltage repeatedly and restarted once (11:21), taking the telemetry unit with it; a time-sync setting then had to be corrected so that its clock stepped to the Raspberry Pi~5's after a restart. From then to the end of the laps the system ran without a restart, and between 11:30 and 12:40 the recorder lost no poll. The Raspberry Pi~5 went off when the vehicle was switched off after the laps, and its data were copied once it was powered again. Removal was not timed, so R13 stays partly met.
