\chapter{Results}
\label{chap:results}

This chapter gives the measured results of the experiments of Section~\ref{sec:experiments}, analysed as in Section~\ref{sec:data-analysis}, against the requirements of Table~\ref{tab:requirements-mapping}. Bench results were measured between 25 September and 22 October 2026; results from the vehicle (the first road session, 19 October) are marked as such. It is structured by requirement, earlier results first and the road session last, and closes with a summary table (Section~\ref{sec:res-summary}). The headline result of each test is given here; the full run histories, per-run breakdowns and longer tables are in Appendix~\ref{app:results}, and the raw evidence is indexed in Appendix~\ref{app:evidence}. Results that the second road session will supply are marked as outstanding.

\section{Inertial acquisition (R1)}
\label{sec:res-acq}

Both IMUs held 200.0 to 200.5\,Hz with no dropped samples in every bench session, including with all subsystems running together and with the 94\,Hz filter of v0.7.4 (Table~\ref{tab:res-acquisition}). The only timing fault was the pause at each flash write. Figure~\ref{fig:res-imu-gaps} shows the longest interval in each 2\,s window: writing every 10\,s (v0.5.0), 30 of 151 windows per IMU had a gap of 33 to 40\,ms; with buffered writes (v0.6.0) only the 5 windows after the five flushes did, and the worst gap elsewhere was 5.9\,ms in both runs. No dropped samples does not mean even spacing: after a pause the missed samples are read about 1\,ms apart, which distorts the jerk of that window. R1 is met on the bench as defined; up to v0.7.4.1 a read that failed on the bus was not detected, so those figures cover the processing path, not the bus.

On the first road session, with about 16 minutes of driving, the drop count stayed at zero on both IMUs, and 3,431 of the 3,437 readable records show both at 200.0 to 200.5\,Hz; one record, while driving, shows a stall of IMU1 of nearly 2\,s whose cause is not known. Of the 5,557 seconds from the first to the last record, 2,093 (38\,\%) have none, lost between the unit and the Raspberry Pi~4 over USB. On the vehicle R1 is therefore partly met.

On 21 and 22 October, on its own battery and the wired link, the rear unit's drop counts rose until a reset: the statistics and ride tasks had stopped on the IMU core, and v0.7.6 moved them (Section~\ref{sec:inv-stall}). On v0.7.6 both nodes ran at 200\,Hz with no drops or read failures, and a 62-minute soak of the rear unit under the fault's conditions had no restarts, drops, read failures or late slots, with the rate outside 199.5 to 200.5\,Hz in 3 of 726 polls (Table~\ref{tab:res-twonode}). The soak shows that v0.7.6 ran through the fault's conditions, not that the trigger was present. With v0.7.8 the unit re-initialised IMU1 by itself after each of four pulls of its supply wire, with no drops and no reset (Section~\ref{sec:impl-health}). R1 is met on both nodes on the bench.

\section{Calibration (R2)}

Before calibration the two IMUs read about 5.7\,\% above and 2.6\,\% below standard gravity at rest; after it both read 9.80 to 9.81\,m/s\textsuperscript{2}. On the target board a later session read 1.3\,\% and 2.9\,\% above gravity with the same factors, which confirms that a single factor holds only in the orientation in which it was measured. On the front node a recalibration on 22 October repeated the first factor within 0.01\,\% (1.0036 and 1.0035), with the board not yet mounted (Section~\ref{sec:resx-cal}). R2 is met at calibration, not yet in the mounted position.

\section{Ride features and fusion (R3)}

The feature self-test and all five fusion self-tests passed at every boot, and in a 150\,s bench session all 149 fused windows combined both sensors with a skew below 1\,ms; under a hand disturbance both sensors rose and decayed together (Section~\ref{sec:resx-ride}). R3 is met: the features are computed and combined as designed, which does not show how they respond to the road or the motor.

On the road session the per-second vibration value separated the states clearly (Figure~\ref{fig:res-road}): a median of 0.03\,m/s\textsuperscript{2} in 2,832 parked seconds and 1.39\,m/s\textsuperscript{2} in 605 driving seconds (95th percentile 3.2, maximum 5.09), with battery current up to 46.9\,A.

\subsection{Run-1 flash log}
\label{sec:res-flashlog}

The unit's own log of the session, read back on 22 October, holds the per-IMU features of one 1\,s window about every 23\,s (1,154 records; Figure~\ref{fig:res-flashlog}, Table~\ref{tab:res-flashlog}). In the 26 records identified as driving by current, $\sigma$ rose from about 0.03 to a median of about 2\,m/s\textsuperscript{2}, peak-to-peak from about 0.17 to 11 to 14\,m/s\textsuperscript{2}, crest factor from 1.01 to about 1.6 and jerk from about 7 to 250\,m/s\textsuperscript{3}. The two sensors, both on the rear unit that day, tracked each other closely: the ratio of their $\sigma$ had a median of 1.06 and a correlation of 0.97, and $D$ a median of 0.074. This shows the size of the response and that two sensors measuring the same motion agreed, not its repeatability, its dependence on speed (there was no GNSS antenna) or how the front and rear compare.

\section{Position (R4)}

The receiver reached a fix 105\,s after a cold start, and over 104 fixes the position was a median of 2.7\,m from a phone's, 95\,\% within 11.0\,m: against the 10\,m criterion the median passes but the 95th percentile does not, and the phone's own error makes this a consistency check. The speed field has not been checked against a known speed. On the road session no GNSS antenna was connected, so no position or speed was recorded; R4 is untested on the vehicle rather than failed (Section~\ref{sec:resx-gnss}).

\section{Battery telemetry (R5)}

Once the unit was moved near the pack (Section~\ref{sec:two-sided}), the BMS link held, and on the road session it held throughout: the current reached 46.9\,A under load (48.03\,A in the unit's own log), the voltage fell from 74.6 to 72.8\,V and the state of charge from 60 to 58\,\%. The pack name gives 24 cells, and 74.7\,V at 60\,\% fits lithium iron phosphate. R5 is partly met: the voltage has not been compared with a meter (Section~\ref{sec:resx-bms}).

\section{Hazard sensing (R6 to R8)}
\label{sec:res-hazard}

On the Raspberry Pi~4, the rear unit, the detector alone ran at 19.5\,frames/s with a median of 102\,ms from sensor readout to result, of which about 52\,ms was readout and image processing and 36\,ms inference (Table~\ref{tab:camera-latency}). With the whole system running it ran at 14.6 to 14.7\,frames/s with a median of 118 to 122\,ms; the cause of the difference has not been isolated. The Raspberry Pi~5 beside the running dashboard reached 19.2\,frames/s with a median of 51 to 54\,ms (Figure~\ref{fig:res-latency}). The three-frame onset adds two frame intervals, so an alert is raised at the detector about 0.26\,s, or 2.2\,m at 30\,km/h, after an object appears. Only the person class has been confirmed on live frames, indoors; R7 is met at the detector on the bench for that class. R6 is met for the pulse itself, timestamped with a spread of under 0.7\,ms, but the offset between the clocks has not been measured, and the median interval of 500.06\,ms means a drift of about 0.4\,s an hour that the alignment must fit. The full hazard-sensing results are in Table~\ref{tab:res-camera}.

\section{Telemetry, logging and recovery (R9, R10)}

The unit published 47 times with no failures, detected a radio loss within 0.8\,s and, from v0.5.3, reconnected about 5\,s after coverage returned; a full read-back recovered all 6,020 stored records (Section~\ref{sec:resx-telemetry}). In the store-and-forward test T15 (Table~\ref{tab:res-sync}, Figure~\ref{fig:res-t15}) the radio was off for 10 minutes; the unit reconnected 5.2\,s after it returned and cleared its 60-record backlog, and every record of the boot was stored once, 24 of them only after a backfill from the unit's log. The public broker acknowledges a record with no subscriber present, so on it the log read-back is the safety net. R9 is met on the bench, including the database.

\section{Driver display (R11, R12)}

On a laptop against the live detector, the camera page showed video within 0.6\,s at 14.2\,frames/s and listed a person alert 0.5\,s after it was opened with the person in view. In live-only mode every page showed ``-{}-'' or ``Unavailable'' with the bridge down, never a simulated value. On the Raspberry Pi~5 the Sensors page showed live distances in the right colours and the banner appeared over the driving page, and the touch panel added no measurable delay (Section~\ref{sec:resx-display}). R11 is met on the bench. R12, the glance demand, has not been measured: no person takes part as a subject (Section~\ref{sec:ethics}), so it is left for future work.

\section{Integration and power}

The chain from the telemetry unit through the Raspberry Pi~4 to the dashboard ran on the bench, and on 19 October the whole system was mounted on the vehicle: both detectors ran (front 17.5 to 22.0\,frames/s, 53 to 102\,ms; rear 14.4 to 14.5\,frames/s, 126 to 151\,ms), both time-of-flight boards answered, both IMUs held 200\,Hz and the Ethernet link ran at 1000\,Mb/s. The Raspberry Pi~4's under-voltage was traced to the modem on its USB port (Section~\ref{sec:inv-power}). On the road session the recorder ran for 108.6 minutes without restarting, though the rear unit stopped answering it for the last 15 minutes, cause not recorded. The estimated added load is about 20\,W from the 72\,V pack while driving, 0.28\,\% of a 7.2\,kWh pack an hour, but enough to empty it in about 15 days if left on, so the system must switch off with the vehicle (Table~\ref{tab:power-budget}, Section~\ref{sec:resx-power}). Only the Raspberry Pi~5's figure is measured.
