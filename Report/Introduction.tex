\section{The vehicle and the problem}

In Dar es Salaam, an electric cargo trike cost 45.5\,\% less to operate than a motorcycle and 86\,\% less than a light car (Section~\ref{sec:lit-trikes}). The TUKZIE Flagship Programme of the University of Cape Town \cite{tukzie2026web} is developing such a vehicle as an energy-aware, secure and autonomy-ready platform \cite{winberg2026} for last-mile logistics in growing cities \cite{unwup2019}. Its testbed is the TUKZIE Rev~0, a 72\,V trike made by Jinpeng \cite{jinpengmanual}. Its hub motor is rated at about 2.5\,kW input \cite{adamsrasara2026}, while the vehicle's plate gives 3.9\,kW output and 42\,km/h (Figure~\ref{fig:vehicle-nameplate}); the two disagree and neither was measured.

When the project began, the trike's Raspberry Pi~5 touchscreen dashboard showed only simulated values (Section~\ref{sec:dashboard-link}). Nothing measured its response to the road or powertrain, read the battery or recorded a journey. Road traffic injuries are a leading cause of death, mostly in low- and middle-income countries \cite{who2023}, so hazard alerts are worth having, but only if the display does not itself distract: US NHTSA guidance limits a single glance away from the road to 2\,s and the glances for one task to 12\,s \cite{nhtsa2013}.

The engineering problem \cite{winberg2026} is to acquire synchronised data reliably on a high-vibration, power-constrained vehicle and tell road-induced motion from the vehicle's own dynamics. Latency must stay bounded, telemetry must survive gaps in coverage, and the result must not block the driver's view or depend on a remote service. The objective is a high-performance embedded computing (HPEC) telemetry unit for the TUKZIE Rev~0, integrated with its existing dashboard. The unit acquires synchronised inertial, battery, positioning and camera data, characterises the vehicle's response on board, keeps telemetry through coverage gaps and shows the driver only essential safety information.

\section{Deliverables and scope change}

The project started from a brief set by the supervisors, and its scope changed as the work showed what the vehicle needed. The brief \cite{winberg2026} asked for (1) an HPEC telemetry unit with a deterministic multi-threaded architecture; (2) a driver interface, originally a windshield head-up display (HUD); (3) synchronisation with a central database over 4G/LTE; and (4) a technical report validating the data and the system. With the supervisor's agreement, the HUD was replaced by integration with the existing dashboard, because a second display would compete for the same attention \cite{zhu2021} (Section~\ref{sec:lit-display}). The title was changed on 2 and 23 October to follow the work. The supervisor then asked for a camera hazard detector, later joined by time-of-flight proximity sensing and, in October, a second camera and sensor board at the rear that warns of approaching objects. The aim is a prototype demonstrated on the vehicle at Technology Readiness Level 6 \cite{kimmel2020}, not a product for unrestricted road use.

\section{Research questions}

The literature search found no system that does all of the following on a light electric cargo trike: fixed-rate inertial acquisition with measured timing and loss, on-board ride features, camera and range fusion, cellular telemetry with local logging and replay, and display on the vehicle's own dashboard (Section~\ref{sec:lit-synthesis}, Table~\ref{tab:gap}). The contribution sought is this integration and its measurement, not any one component:

\begin{enumerate}
\item[RQ1] How accurately and repeatably can a low-cost edge-processing system characterise the trike's dynamic response to road- and powertrain-induced excitation in real time, using inertial sensors on a 72\,V electric cargo trike?
\item[RQ2] Which information hierarchy, layout and update strategy can present safety-critical telemetry and hazard alerts on the vehicle's existing dashboard while limiting visual clutter and driver distraction?
\item[RQ3] What end-to-end latency, data-loss and recovery performance can be achieved across sensor acquisition, edge processing, local logging and 4G/LTE synchronisation?
\item[RQ4] Which time- and frequency-domain features most effectively separate road-induced vibration from repeatable motor and powertrain vibration?
\end{enumerate}

In RQ1, accuracy means the accuracy of the instrument: its sampling rate, sample loss, calibration and feature computation. No reference profile or reference accelerometer was available for the ride itself, so the road part of RQ1 is answered by repeatability.

Each has an acceptance criterion fixed before its test, except T1, which was run before the procedures were written. Where the literature gives a figure, the criterion uses it: the 160\,Hz sampling floor is the Nyquist rate of the 80\,Hz band of ISO~2631-1 \cite{iso2631}, the 2\,s glance limit is NHTSA's \cite{nhtsa2013}, and the store-and-forward criteria follow the completeness, duplicate and recovery measures of Section~\ref{sec:lit-sync}. \section{Scope and limitations}

Three parts were delivered, bench-tested and run on the vehicle, with GNSS and LTE publishing on the bench only. They are the ESP32-S3 telemetry unit (IMU, GNSS, battery-management system over Bluetooth Low Energy, LTE, local logging, replay into a database), joined on 22 October by a front IMU node; forward and rear camera units, each with a pretrained detector and three time-of-flight sensors (fusion not yet validated); and dashboard integration. In scope but not delivered, so future work (Chapter~\ref{chap:recs}): the SEN55 air-quality sensor, damaged in wiring (Section~\ref{sec:environmental-sensing}); the VL53L5CX time-of-flight sensor, not received; the motor Hall tap; and LTE publishing from the moving vehicle. Road-surface characterisation here means the vehicle's ride response and bump events, not a road profile measured as a roughness index \cite{fhwa2016} (Section~\ref{sec:lit-roughness}). The limitations are one vehicle and one hardware set, an advisory-only pretrained detector at automation level 0 \cite{saej3016}, calibration not rechecked after mounting, short low-speed vehicle sessions and mostly bench results. This scope follows the brief's order, a working baseline before machine learning or autonomy \cite{winberg2026}, and the staged validation of a prototype towards Technology Readiness Level 6 \cite{kimmel2020}.
