\chapter{Dashboard Interface Design}
\label{app:interface}

This appendix records how the driver's dashboard on the Raspberry Pi~5 was redesigned between 11 and 12 October: the steps, the rules the design follows, and how each step was checked. The data path behind the pages is described in Section~\ref{sec:dashboard-page}. The refinement was deployed to the Pi~5 on 12 October (commit \texttt{5baa5f2}) and the three-planes version on 13 October (commit \texttt{864ce27}). The dashboard ran on the vehicle during the road session of 19 October, but its pages were not measured there, and glance times have not been measured.

\section{Starting point and constraints}

The inherited dashboard (Figure~\ref{fig:ui-before}) is the vacation-work teams' PySide6 application. It has eight pages: Driving, Analytics, Diagnostics, Navigation, Front camera, Sensors, Charging and Settings.

Every SW-7 change reaches it as a patch with an exact anchor, applied to a copy (\texttt{DashboardIntegration/\allowbreak{}tests/\allowbreak{}dashboard\_\allowbreak{}patches.py}), so the team's own copy is never edited. Each stage of the redesign has an environment switch that restores the previous look exactly, and a test checks that it does.

Four constraints shaped the design:
\begin{itemize}
\item it is read at a glance while driving, in sun and at night;
\item it is touched on a moving vehicle, possibly with gloves;
\item it must keep live data and simulated data visibly apart;
\item it must change only the presentation, never the telemetry, routing or sensor logic.
\end{itemize}

\section{Design process}

The redesign was done in six steps. Each step was written as a brief in \texttt{Planning/} and checked by tests and screenshots before the next began.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Layout pass.} A page-by-page review set minimum sizes:
  \begin{itemize}
  \item 64\,px touch targets;
  \item driver-facing values of at least 32\,px;
  \item a 48\,px status bar with 16\,px text, against 38 and 11\,px before.
  \end{itemize}
  The pass also fixed real faults. The Charging page showed 79\% while the status bar showed 80\%, because one truncated and the other rounded.
\item \emph{Data ownership and page merge.} An audit found the same data shown in several places:
  \begin{itemize}
  \item battery percentage in seven places;
  \item link health on two pages;
  \item two different arrival estimates on the map at once;
  \item two unlabelled outside temperatures.
  \end{itemize}
  The student chose to keep the pages but remove the duplicates. Each value now has one home page and appears elsewhere only as a glance value that opens it. The Front camera and Sensors pages were merged into one page, Surroundings, leaving seven pages. Drive became the glance page, with tiles for the next turn, the surroundings and the range.
\item \emph{Paper theme.} The student supplied a reference with paper and ink, one terracotta colour for anything that can be tapped, and very large or very small type. Live weather (Open-Meteo) was added, along with a night variant that switches at sunset.
\item \emph{Refinement.} A second brief asked for restraint:
  \begin{itemize}
  \item IBM Plex Sans for the interface and Plex Mono for readings;
  \item fine borders and small radii in place of floating cards;
  \item a flat navigation bar;
  \item the weather inline in the status bar, with no box;
  \item status that never relies on colour alone;
  \item a dark mode designed in its own right;
  \item the team's battery animation kept, in the new colours.
  \end{itemize}
\item \emph{Three planes.} The student found the refined pages too flat and asked for depth again, after a product poster with a huge word behind the product. Each page now has three planes:
  \begin{itemize}
  \item at the back, one very large word in Archivo Black (OFL), at 14\,\% ink by day and lifted charcoal at night: DRIVE, CHARGE, SETUP;
  \item in the middle, a cut-out of the TUKZIE concept render that overlaps the word;
  \item at the front, the readings and controls, unchanged in function.
  \end{itemize}
  The Charging page returned to the team's original composition, a ring around the vehicle outline with rising bubbles, redrawn in the paper colours and animated only while the page is shown. Where no space was free (Map, Diagnostics) the page has no render. A test checks that the whole word stays readable in the open space above the tiles.
\item \emph{Two cameras.} With the second camera and time-of-flight board on the Raspberry Pi~5 (Section~\ref{sec:two-sided}), Surroundings gained a Front/Rear switch that moves the picture, alerts, distances and hazards together, a mirrored rear view, and a banner that labels each warning by side and shows the time to contact of an approaching object (Figure~\ref{fig:two-sided}). A test with four fake servers checks the switch, a side going offline, the mirroring and the banner, and that the off switch restores the single-camera page exactly.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Design rules}

Table~\ref{tab:ui-rules} lists the rules the pages follow. Two are checked automatically over every page: no non-interactive element uses the tap colour, and every text size comes from the type scale.

\centering
\caption{Interface design rules and their reasons.}
\label{tab:ui-rules}
\small
\begin{tabular}{|p{3.6cm}|p{5.6cm}|p{5.2cm}|}
\hline
\textbf{Rule} & \textbf{Applied as} & \textbf{Reason} \\ \hline
One tap colour & Terracotta only on buttons, toggles, sliders, links and the selected page; checked over every page & The driver can tell what responds to touch without reading \\ \hline
Status not by colour alone & A word with every status mark, for example ``Left, close'' or ``Unavailable'' & Readable in glare and by drivers with colour-vision deficiency \\ \hline
Unavailable is not zero & ``--'' and ``Unavailable'' in muted grey, never 0 & A missing value must not look like a valid reading \\ \hline
Live against simulated & Source chip (LIVE, REPLAY, NO DATA, SIMULATED) and a strip on every page during a simulated drive & Simulated data must never be mistaken for the vehicle's \\ \hline
One hero value per page & Speed, odometer, nearest obstacle, battery, turn distance at 120 to 180\,px; the rest at 13, 16 or 22\,px & A clear hierarchy at a glance \\ \hline
Tabular figures & IBM Plex Mono for changing numbers & Values do not shift as their digit count changes \\ \hline
Touch targets & At least 64\,px & Use on a moving vehicle \\ \hline
One home per value & Other pages show a glance value that opens the home page & Removes duplicated and conflicting figures \\ \hline
Night variant & Charcoal surfaces, the same layout and tap colour, from sunset to sunrise & A white screen dazzles at night \\ \hline

\section{Voice commands (built 25 October, off by default)}
\label{sec:ui-voice}

The vehicle has no gear signal and the driver should not have to look and tap, so the dashboard can also take spoken commands (\texttt{DashboardIntegration/\allowbreak{}sw7\_voice\_control.py}, switched on with \texttt{TUKZIE\_SW7\_VOICE=1}, not yet deployed). They change only what is shown; nothing controls the vehicle:
\begin{itemize}
\item ``home'' shows Driving, ``map'' the map;
\item ``reverse'' or ``rear camera'' shows the rear side of Surroundings, and ``front camera'' the front;
\item since there is no gear signal, the Driving page returns by itself after 45\,s on the rear view, or once the speed has stayed at 8\,km/h or more for 3\,s, or on ``done'';
\item ``map destination Rondebosch'' (or ``go to'', ``take me to'') searches the place, reads out the first three options, and takes ``first'', ``second'' or ``third'' as the destination.
\end{itemize}
Recognition is offline (Vosk, a small English model), so it works without coverage, and audio is processed in memory and never stored. Each action is confirmed through the team's voice output. A heard place name close in spelling to a known Cape Town place is corrected before the search, because the model hears ``Rondebosch'' as ``round bosh''. The offscreen test passes all of these steps, including the automatic return and the choice of the second option against a stand-in search service.

On the bench, 18 commands spoken by the two Windows synthetic voices (so no person was recorded) were recognised and parsed (\texttt{BenchTest/\allowbreak{}voice\_recognition\_check.py}, output in \texttt{BenchTest/\allowbreak{}logs/\allowbreak{}voice\_2026-10-25}):
\begin{itemize}
\item clean: 17 and 13 of 18 understood;
\item with white noise at 10\,dB signal-to-noise ratio: 14 and 13;
\item at 0\,dB: 5 and 9;
\item recognition took a median of 0.7 to 0.9\,s per phrase on the laptop.
\end{itemize}
Short words were sometimes confused (``back camera'' heard as ``that camera''), and loud noise defeats it. Synthetic voices are clearer than a driver, and white noise is not road noise, so these are upper bounds. A test with the driver on the road needs a participant and is outside the approval. A command-only vocabulary and a microphone close to the driver are the next steps.

\section{Verification}

The dashboard's test suite runs the patched application off screen against a copy of the team's source. It covers 15 test files:
\begin{itemize}
\item live data;
\item the map;
\item simulated-drive synchronisation;
\item search;
\item the analytics carousel;
\item the four layout tests;
\item the six style tests, including one that checks the back word stays readable and no render covers a driving value.
\end{itemize}

All passed on the final merged build on 13 October. The style tests check:
\begin{itemize}
\item the tap-colour and type-scale rules;
\item that the bundled fonts load;
\item that the weather item has no border or background;
\item every page in both themes, with no clipped labels;
\item that the battery animation runs.
\end{itemize}

Recorded widget trees and pixels show that the previous look is unchanged when the redesign is switched off. Paint times measured on the laptop were below 15\,ms per frame. The charging animation takes 1.0 to 1.5\,ms per frame, and runs only while its page is visible.

Two of the checks caught faults during the redesign:
\begin{itemize}
\item an off-screen test that reset the status bar's height hid the obstacle pill;
\item a test-cleanup step had been deleting the shared copy of the team's source between runs.
\end{itemize}

Not measured, and so limitations of this design: paint times and the fonts on the Raspberry Pi~5's panel (the touch panel's own delay was measured, Chapter~\ref{chap:results}); contrast and legibility in daylight; and glance durations on the vehicle, which need a participant and so an ethics amendment. Each is a recommendation in Chapter~\ref{chap:recs}.
