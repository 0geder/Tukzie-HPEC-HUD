\chapter{Methodology}
\label{chap:method}

Chapter~\ref{chap:lit} ended with a bounded gap (Section~\ref{sec:lit-synthesis}, Table~\ref{tab:gap}) and two findings: edge architectures are rarely reported by jitter, queue behaviour or outage recovery, and response-based ride measures depend on the vehicle, speed and mounting. This chapter sets out how RQ1 to RQ4 are answered under those findings: the approach and its phases, the sites, the experiments, data collection and analysis, and ethics and safety, summarised in Table~\ref{tab:methods}. What was built is in Chapter~\ref{chap:design}.

\section{Research approach}
\label{sec:approach}

The project is engineering design research: a prototype is built and measured against criteria set before each test, through the thirteen requirements of Table~\ref{tab:requirements-mapping}. The work follows a V-model (Figure~\ref{fig:vmodel}). The left arm defines the system (research questions, requirements and criteria, design); the right arm checks it at the same levels from the bottom up (start-up checks for each interface, bench tests for each requirement, vehicle tests for what only the vehicle can show). The gap concerns measured behaviour, so each design stage needs a test that produces a number; faults often appear only when parts are combined, so each interface is checked before the next is added; and Technology Readiness Level 6 \cite{kimmel2020} requires bench validation of each part before testing in the relevant environment. Criteria come from the literature where it gives one: the 80\,Hz upper limit of the ISO~2631-1 band for the sampling rate \cite{iso2631}, a 2\,s single-glance target for the display \cite{nhtsa2013}, and the completeness, duplicate and recovery measures of Section~\ref{sec:lit-sync}.

\section{Site selection}
\label{sec:site-selection}

Most tests were run on the bench in the laboratory at UCT (Figure~\ref{fig:bench-setup}), which allows one change at a time, repeated runs and a laptop beside the hardware. The GNSS fix needs open sky, so it was tested on a rooftop (T3); the battery link needs the vehicle's pack (T7). The TUKZIE Rev~0 is the only testbed, so vehicle tests use one vehicle and one set of hardware, on rated stands with the drive wheel raised or in a closed area. No person takes part as a subject, so no participants were selected (Section~\ref{sec:ethics}).

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:verification}
\label{sec:experiments}

The experiments follow the right arm of the V-model (Figure~\ref{fig:vmodel}, phases 4 to 6). Each interface is checked on its own, each requirement is then tested on the bench against its criterion, and the whole system is finally tested on the vehicle. Where a fault had more than one possible cause, a deciding test separated them (Section~\ref{sec:investigations}, Table~\ref{tab:investigations}).

\subsection{Bench and integration tests}
\label{sec:bench-tests}

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Start-up checks} (phase 4): pass or fail checks of each interface (Table~\ref{tab:startup-checks}, Appendix~\ref{app:wiring}); all passed, and the faults they exposed are in Table~\ref{tab:faults}.
\item \emph{Bench acceptance tests} (phase 5): one or more per requirement, with procedures and criteria in \texttt{BenchTest/\allowbreak{}TEST\_\allowbreak{}PROCEDURES.md} (T1 to T15 telemetry unit, C1 to C11 camera units, B1 to B5 bench tools and dashboard) and raw output indexed in Appendix~\ref{app:evidence}. T14 on the Raspberry Pis, C5, C8 and the tape measurements of C9 and C11 have not been run.
\item \emph{Integration tests} (phase 5): the whole chain on the bench, which exposed the Raspberry Pi~4's under-voltage from the modem (Section~\ref{sec:inv-power}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Vehicle tests}
\label{sec:full-system-integration}
\label{sec:field-test-design}

The vehicle integration and test plan (SW7-VIT-001) has a stationary phase with the drive wheel raised (mounting, calibration, timing, synchronisation, a powertrain baseline at three motor settings, BMS against a meter, GNSS and LTE static tests) and a phase on the ground (repeatability, speed dependence, coast-down separation of the powertrain, obstacle response). Sessions with 72\,V energised need a second person, rated stands and chocks, and the mounting must be removable, with no drilling, and the vehicle back to standard in about 20 minutes (R13).

A recorder on the Raspberry Pi~5 starts at boot and saves every reply of its sources twice a second to one file per session: the front time-of-flight, fusion and alerts locally, and over the Ethernet cable the rear unit's record and alerts; from 22 October also the front inertial node. Runs are marked by lines in a marker file, with times on a paper run sheet as a backup. No signal gives the gear or direction, so each second is labelled afterwards from battery current, the vibration value and speed.

\emph{First road session (19 October).} With the whole system mounted (Appendix~\ref{app:road1}), Yusuf Vadwa drove the vehicle. It was recorded from 17:15 to 19:04, with about 16 minutes of driving in 26 segments between 18:15 and 18:49. The venue was the enclosed parking lot outside the Menzies Building, after 17:00, with no traffic; the route and weather were not recorded. The firmware (v0.7.4) kept no raw samples, no GNSS antenna was fitted, and one run marker was written. The unit's flash log of the session, which kept one second of features from each IMU roughly every 23\,s (1,154 records), was read out on 22 October.

\emph{Second road session.} The lab part (23 October) ran stand runs and recalibrated both nodes in place; the road part was planned as counterbalanced passes over three marked sections with side video, tap slates and meters as ground truth (Section~\ref{sec:impl-run2}). As driven on 27 October it was laps at 10, 15 and 20\,km/h on a smooth and a rough loop, with stops between laps and no typed markers, video or meters: the pass windows were derived afterwards from the phone's GNSS speed and the battery current, and the analysis was otherwise run as written beforehand, except that the ICC rows were 8\,m ground cells placed by GNSS, not 5\,m section bins.

\section{Data collection}
\label{sec:data-collection}

A result is only as good as its record, so data collection was designed into the system. Every sample, record and alert carries what is needed to check it later, and every file is kept and indexed. Section~\ref{sec:what-recorded} describes what is recorded and how the clocks are aligned, and Section~\ref{sec:data-availability} where the data can be found.

\subsection{What is recorded}
\label{sec:what-recorded}

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Inertial samples.} Each sample carries a sensor identifier, a sequence number and a microsecond timestamp, and every 2\,s a statistics task reports each sensor's rate, interval range and drops (Section~\ref{sec:impl-fw}). Up to v0.7.4.1 only the 1\,s features were kept; from v0.7.5 every sample also goes to the Raspberry Pi as a CRC-checked line, with read-failure and stuck-sample counters.
\item \emph{Front and rear nodes.} From 22 October each IMU has its own ESP32-S3 (Section~\ref{sec:impl-front}), both on v0.7.8 since 15:00 that day. The rear node, on its own battery, sends to the Raspberry Pi~4 over a wired UART at 460\,800\,baud, the front node to the Raspberry Pi~5 over USB at 921\,600\,baud, and each Pi saves its node's raw stream to file. From 26 October the rear IMU has its own bare ESP32-S3 on the Raspberry Pi~4's USB, and the UART carries only the telemetry unit.
\item \emph{Clocks.} Each node drives a 500\,ms pulse on a wire to its Pi (rear GPIO15 to Pi~4 pin~11, front GPIO5 to Pi~5 pin~11), so its clock can be mapped to the Pi's by a straight-line fit. The Pis share one time base through chrony over the Ethernet cable, Pi~5 serving (\texttt{DashboardIntegration/\allowbreak{}deploy/\allowbreak{}setup\_\allowbreak{}sw7\_\allowbreak{}chrony.sh}), checked by taps on a common plate. On 22 October both pulses were logged on their Pis by services (edges every 499.7 to 500.3\,ms), and chrony synchronised the Raspberry Pi~4 to the Raspberry Pi~5 with an error bound of $\pm 79\,\mu$s (offset estimate 0.5\,$\mu$s); the tap check on the vehicle was not run.
\item \emph{Logs and database.} Bench serial output is saved under \texttt{BenchTest/logs/}. Every record is written to flash whatever the coverage and read back with an indexed, length-checked dump (Sections~\ref{sec:local-logging} and~\ref{sec:inv-json}); the ingest stores each record once per device, boot and sequence number and can backfill from the local log (Section~\ref{sec:db-sync}).
\item \emph{Camera, fusion, power.} Frame latency from the sensor timestamp is reported every 5\,s as median, 95th percentile and maximum, and each alert is logged with its class, band, confidence, time and status, and an approaching-object alert also with its side, time to contact and track number (Section~\ref{sec:hazard-detection}). Fusion distances are logged only for validation sessions. Loggers record each Pi's supply, throttle flags and temperature every 2 or 5\,s (Section~\ref{sec:inv-power}).
\end{itemize}
Every record is kept unedited under \texttt{Report/evidence/} or \texttt{BenchTest/logs/}, indexed in Appendix~\ref{app:evidence}, and each day's lab actions are in \texttt{Planning/lab-notebook.md}.

\subsection{Data availability}
\label{sec:data-availability}

The source code, firmware, test scripts, bench logs and figure data are in the project repository, \url{https://github.com/0geder/Tukzie-HPEC-HUD}. Its \texttt{DATA\_INDEX.md} lists every data file behind a number, figure or table in this report with its date, test identifier, the part of the report it supports, size, SHA-256 checksum and whether git tracks it; \texttt{tools/\allowbreak{}build\_\allowbreak{}data\_\allowbreak{}index.py} rebuilds it (182 files on 22 October). The road-session recordings and alert logs are too large for the repository; they are held by the author, available on request, and checkable against the index. Photographs and video that show people other than the author are withheld for privacy (Section~\ref{sec:ethics}); the index lists them with their checksums, and the report uses only stills in which faces and the number plate are cropped or blurred (Appendix~\ref{app:road1}).

\section{Data analysis}
\label{sec:data-analysis}

Each record is turned into a figure comparable with the acceptance criterion of Table~\ref{tab:requirements-mapping}, fixed before the test. Chapter~\ref{chap:results} reports the requirements in the same order, from acquisition (Section~\ref{sec:res-acq}) to integration, and gives each a status in Table~\ref{tab:results-summary}. Results not measured are not estimated. Section~\ref{sec:bench-analysis} gives the analysis of the bench tests and the first road session, and Section~\ref{sec:road-analysis} the analysis written for RQ1 and RQ4.

\subsection{Bench tests and the first road session}
\label{sec:bench-analysis}

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Timing.} Latency and interval distributions are skewed and an alert is limited by its slow cases, so they are summarised by the median, 95th percentile and maximum, not the mean, answering the review's finding that edge systems are rarely reported by jitter. Acquisition is judged by rate, drops and gaps over 10\,ms placed against the flash writes (R1).
\item \emph{Position, pulse, store-and-forward.} Fixes are compared with a phone's position (R4) and pulse intervals with 500 $\pm$ 20\,ms (R6). Records are counted by device, boot and sequence number for missing records, duplicates, replays and backlog time (\texttt{Server/sync\_report.py}), and the database is compared with the read-back log (R9).
\item \emph{Road session 1.} The bridge repeats its last record and the recorder polls twice a second, so records are first reduced to one per sequence number (Appendix~\ref{app:ai} records the correction this needed). A second counts as driving when the current exceeds 1\,A or the vibration value 0.3, with segments under 10\,s apart joined.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Road characterisation pipeline}
\label{sec:road-analysis}

RQ1 and RQ4 need repeated traversals, raw samples and a speed reference, which the first session lacked. Their analysis was written before the second session as one command that processes a session folder without hand steps and records any analysis it cannot run, with the reason. Its 61 tests pass on synthetic signals with known answers, it reproduces the figures of the first session, and vehicle-dependent values such as the wheelbase have no default.

The analysis computes, per node, the dynamic vertical acceleration from the gravity direction, band-passed from 2 to 40\,Hz for the ride features and 0.5 to 80\,Hz for the powertrain analysis, with clipped samples flagged; the ISO~2631-1 weighted acceleration $a_w$ and vibration dose value \cite{iso2631} beside the time-domain features and $I_v$; repeatability over passes as a coefficient of variation (CV) and an intraclass correlation (ICC) over 5\,m bins of each section with 95\,\% intervals, against proposed criteria of a CV of at most 10\,\% and an ICC of at least 0.75 (RQ1); the speed dependence of $I_v$ over the three speeds, against the hypothesis, set before the run, that it is speed-invariant (RQ1); the front-to-rear delay from the cross-correlation of the two nodes over full-width features, giving speed without GNSS; the powertrain lines from Welch spectra of the stand runs and their share of the road spectrum in powered and coasting passes (RQ4); and a pothole detector against a pothole defined, as a project choice, as a depression at least 25\,mm deep and 150\,mm across, reported as counts of hits, misses and false alarms, since under 1\,km of road cannot estimate a false-alarm rate. The $W_k$ filter follows its published analogue form \cite{rimell2007} and matches a second published table within 0.005\,dB at 37 frequencies \cite{virtins2024}. Each method, with its parameters and criteria, is in Section~\ref{sec:impl-road}.

\section{Ethics and safety}
\label{sec:ethics}

The study was approved with conditions by the Engineering and Built Environment Research Ethics Committee on 6 October 2026, reference EBE/03305/2026, under the project's original title (Appendix~\ref{app:ethics}); the title changes of 2, 23 and 26 October did not change its questions, and the course convenor confirmed that no new application was needed. The approval rests on there being no human subjects and lapses on 31 December 2026. Its conditions were met: the full proposal was given to the supervisor, and the laboratory owner signed the physical risk assessment before the road session of 19 October. Before it only the equipment's own behaviour was recorded on the bench, with the author the only person in camera view; the first data collection, the road session of 19 October, followed it.

No person takes part as a subject: nobody is asked to do anything, and no glance or driver measurement is made. On the road sessions the vehicle is driven by Yusuf, the laboratory owner, who is responsible for it and authorised to drive it; departmental rules do not allow the author to. He conducts no research and is not measured, and the supervisor agreed to this arrangement. The cameras see people, so what is kept is limited by design: frames are processed in memory and never stored, the live view stays on the vehicle's own network, and each alert keeps only its class, distance band, confidence, time, status, side, time to contact and a tracking number that restarts at every start-up. There is no face or number-plate recognition, and warnings are advisory. Frames kept as evidence show the author or have other people's faces blurred, and road-session video stays off the repository, with bystanders and number plates blurred before any copy is shown.

The topic's ethics notes required a revised application before machine learning was used; the approved application stated that it might be used once the baseline worked, so the approval covers the pretrained detector and the pothole network trained on public datasets (Section~\ref{sec:hazard-detection}). The project collects no training data.

During bench tests the records, including the unit's position on campus, went to a public test broker (Section~\ref{sec:mqtt-networking}). Since v0.7.5 (both nodes run v0.7.8) the published record carries no position. From the vehicle, 153 records reached the database on 27 October (Section~\ref{sec:res-telemetry}).
