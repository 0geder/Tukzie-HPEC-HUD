\chapter{Conclusions}
\label{chap:conclusions}

The project set out to give the TUKZIE Rev~0 an embedded telemetry unit that measures the trike's response to the road and its own powertrain, warns the driver of hazards on the existing dashboard, and keeps its data through gaps in coverage (Chapter~\ref{chap:intro}). The approach was engineering design research along a V-model: thirteen requirements with criteria fixed before each test, bench tests for each part, then vehicle tests for what only the vehicle can show (Chapter~\ref{chap:method}). The research questions are answered first, from the evidence of Chapter~\ref{chap:results}, followed by the requirements and deliverables, the contribution and the limits of the evidence.

\section{Answers to the research questions}
\label{sec:conc-rqs}

\paragraph{RQ1: accuracy and repeatability of the dynamic-response measurement.} The measurement chain is sound on the bench (Sections~\ref{sec:res-acq} and~\ref{sec:res-cal}). Both IMUs were read at 200\,Hz with no queue overflows while every other subsystem ran, but the once-a-minute flash write interrupts reading for up to 44\,ms, and the catch-up reads that follow are new readings, not the missed instants. Calibration brought both to 9.80 to 9.81\,m/s\textsuperscript{2}, but only in the orientation calibrated: with the bench factors the parked vehicle read 10.04 and 10.34. The features are computed correctly. On the first road session the drop count stayed at zero, but one stall of about 2\,s lost some 400 samples that the count does not show (Section~\ref{sec:res-road1}); Section~\ref{sec:disc-acq} discusses what such loss costs. RQ1 is therefore partly answered: the instrument's rate, calibration and features are verified on the bench, and its repeatability on the road rests on the second session.

\paragraph{RQ2: presenting safety-critical information on the existing dashboard.} A sparse presentation, following the glance and clutter limits of Section~\ref{sec:lit-display}, was built on the vehicle's own dashboard. A camera page shows the live view and active alerts, a Sensors page shows distances and fused hazards, and a banner over every page shows an immediate hazard. Only live data are shown, and a record more than 3\,s old is shown as absent. The delay from an object's appearance to the screen and the driver's glance time were not measured. RQ2 is answered (Sections~\ref{sec:res-display} and~\ref{sec:disc-display}) for the design and its legibility, which puts the warnings' text below ISO~15008, but not for its effect on the driver.

\paragraph{RQ3: latency, data loss and recovery.} On the bench (Section~\ref{sec:res-telemetry}), acquisition loses no samples and pauses only at the once-a-minute flash write (33 to 44\,ms). The camera returns a result a median of about 120\,ms after readout on the Raspberry Pi~4 with the whole system running, and 51 to 54\,ms on the Raspberry Pi~5. The unit reconnects about 5\,s after coverage returns, and after a 10-minute outage every logged record was stored once in the database, the store-and-forward behaviour Section~\ref{sec:lit-sync} calls for. On the vehicle, the USB link to the recorder lost 38\,\% of the per-second records, which led to the wired serial link. RQ3 is answered on the bench. Latency to the database and completeness on the moving vehicle were not measured.

\paragraph{RQ4: features that separate road from powertrain vibration.} The separation follows the approach of Section~\ref{sec:lit-signal-processing}. The time-domain features are verified on the bench. The frequency-domain analysis is built and tested on synthetic signals: it finds the powertrain lines in stand runs and their share of the road spectrum in powered and coasting passes. The stand runs of 23 October put the powertrain's energy between about 40 and 70\,Hz at the rear, with the same lines at the front (Sections~\ref{sec:res-stands} and~\ref{sec:disc-ride}). Its share of the road spectrum rests on the road part of the second session.

\section{Requirements and deliverables}
\label{sec:conc-reqs}

The brief asked for four deliverables. Three were produced and the driver interface in a changed form; the environmental and air-quality payload named in the first was not delivered, because its sensor was damaged in wiring (Section~\ref{sec:scope}). The telemetry unit samples its inertial sensors at a fixed rate under a deterministic task structure and reads the battery and position alongside them. The driver interface became part of the trike's existing dashboard instead of a windshield head-up display, a change agreed with the supervisors because a second display would compete for the driver's attention. Telemetry reaches a database over 4G/LTE, and what cannot be sent is logged on the unit and replayed later. The fourth deliverable is this report.

The thirteen requirements give a finer measure (Table~\ref{tab:results-summary}). Five are met on the bench: fixed-rate acquisition, calibration, the on-board features, store-and-forward telemetry and the link to the dashboard (R1 to R3, R9 and R10). Seven are met in part, most often because a test on the moving vehicle is still to come (R4 to R8, R11 and R13). The last, the driver's glance time (R12), was not tested, since it needs a participant and an ethics amendment.

\section{Contribution}
\label{sec:conc-contribution}

Against the gap of Section~\ref{sec:lit-synthesis}, the project delivers an integrated, low-cost architecture for a three-wheeled electric vehicle. It combines fixed-rate inertial acquisition with measured timing and loss, on-board ride features, camera detection fused with time-of-flight ranging, cellular telemetry with local logging and replay into a database, and presentation on the existing dashboard. Sensing and a closing-object warning work at both ends. Every part has been measured on the bench except the ranging accuracy, the time to contact and the delay to the screen, and the whole system has run on the vehicle. Integration exposed faults no subsystem test showed (Sections~\ref{sec:res-integration} and~\ref{sec:disc-power}), above all the under-voltage from powering the LTE modem from the Raspberry Pi. The work has reached a prototype run on the vehicle, short of the demonstration in a relevant environment set as the target. 

\section{Limits of the evidence and the way forward}
\label{sec:conc-limits}

The results come from one vehicle, one bench set-up and, at the time of writing, one road session without a speed reference. No reference accelerometer or measured road profile was available, so road accuracy rests on repeatability (Section~\ref{sec:requirements-mapping}), and no driver's glances were measured, so nothing is claimed about distraction.

This project ends with a working prototype on the vehicle and a measured account of each of its parts. Chapter~\ref{chap:recs} looks ahead: it recommends, from building and testing the system, the steps that would make its data dependable on the road, then suggests further directions and projects for the TUKZIE's sensing.
