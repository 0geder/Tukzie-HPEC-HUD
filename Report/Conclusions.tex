\chapter{Conclusions}
\label{chap:conclusions}

The project set out to give the TUKZIE Rev~0 an embedded telemetry unit that measures the trike's response to the road and its own powertrain, warns the driver of hazards on the existing dashboard, and keeps its data through gaps in coverage (Chapter~\ref{chap:intro}). The approach was engineering design research along a V-model: thirteen requirements with criteria fixed before each test, bench tests for each part, then vehicle tests for what only the vehicle can show (Chapter~\ref{chap:method}). Each research question is answered here from the evidence in Chapter~\ref{chap:results}, keeping what has been shown apart from what still depends on the vehicle; the brief's deliverables, the contribution and the limits of the evidence follow.

\paragraph{RQ1: accuracy and repeatability of the dynamic-response measurement.} The measurement chain is sound on the bench (Sections~\ref{sec:res-acq} and~\ref{sec:res-cal}). Two IMUs are sampled at a fixed 200\,Hz with no lost samples while every other subsystem runs; they were on one board for the bench tests and on one node each from 22 October. Calibration brought both to 9.80 to 9.81\,m/s\textsuperscript{2}, but only in the orientation calibrated: with the bench factors the parked vehicle read 10.04 and 10.34. The features are computed correctly. On the first road session the drop count stayed at zero, but one stall of about 2\,s lost some 400 samples that the count does not show (Section~\ref{sec:res-road1}); Section~\ref{sec:disc-acq} discusses what such loss costs. That session had no speed reference, raw samples or run markers. RQ1 is answered for the instrument on the bench. On the road, its answer rests on the second session.

\paragraph{RQ2: presenting safety-critical information on the existing dashboard.} A sparse presentation, following the glance and clutter limits of Section~\ref{sec:lit-display}, was built on the vehicle's own dashboard. A camera page shows the live view and active alerts, a Sensors page shows distances and fused hazards, and a banner over every page shows an immediate hazard. Only live data are shown, and a record more than 3\,s old is shown as absent. One page switches between front and rear, and the banner names the side of each warning and puts approaching objects first. The delay from an object's appearance to the screen and the driver's glance time were not measured. RQ2 is answered (Sections~\ref{sec:res-display} and~\ref{sec:disc-display}) for the design and its legibility, which puts the warnings' text below ISO~15008, but not for its effect on the driver.

\paragraph{RQ3: latency, data loss and recovery.} On the bench (Section~\ref{sec:res-telemetry}), acquisition loses no samples and pauses only at the once-a-minute flash write (33 to 44\,ms). The camera returns a result a median of about 120\,ms after readout on the Raspberry Pi~4 with the whole system running, and 51 to 54\,ms on the Raspberry Pi~5. The unit reconnects about 5\,s after coverage returns, and after a 10-minute outage every logged record was stored once in the database, the store-and-forward behaviour Section~\ref{sec:lit-sync} calls for. On the vehicle, the USB link to the recorder lost 38\,\% of the per-second records, which led to the wired serial link. RQ3 is answered on the bench. Latency to the database and completeness on the moving vehicle were not measured.

\paragraph{RQ4: features that separate road from powertrain vibration.} The separation follows the approach of Section~\ref{sec:lit-signal-processing}. The time-domain features are verified on the bench. The frequency-domain analysis is built and tested on synthetic signals: it finds the powertrain lines in stand runs and their share of the road spectrum in powered and coasting passes. The stand runs of 23 October put the powertrain's energy between about 40 and 70\,Hz at the rear, with the same lines at the front (Sections~\ref{sec:res-stands} and~\ref{sec:disc-ride}). Its share of the road spectrum rests on the road part of the second session.

\outstanding{from run 2 (road part, 26 October): RQ1 answered with the CV and ICC of the per-pass weighted acceleration over repeated passes and the speed dependence of $I_v$; RQ4 answered with the powertrain lines found on the stands and their share of the road spectrum.}

\paragraph{Deliverables and requirements.} All four deliverables of the brief were produced: the telemetry unit, with fixed-rate acquisition on two nodes and a deterministic task structure; the driver interface, as integration with the existing dashboard in place of a head-up display; synchronisation with a database over 4G/LTE, with local logging and replay; and this report. Of the thirteen requirements (Table~\ref{tab:results-summary}), five are met on the bench (R1, R2, R3, R9, R10), seven are partly met (R4 to R8, R11, R13), and one, the glance time (R12), was not tested because it needs a participant.

\paragraph{Overall.} Against the gap of Section~\ref{sec:lit-synthesis}, the project delivers an integrated, low-cost architecture for a three-wheeled electric vehicle. It combines fixed-rate inertial acquisition with measured timing and loss, on-board ride features, camera detection fused with time-of-flight ranging, cellular telemetry with local logging and replay into a database, and presentation on the existing dashboard. Sensing and a closing-object warning work at both ends. Every part has been measured on the bench except the ranging accuracy, the time to contact and the delay to the screen, and the whole system has run on the vehicle. Integration exposed faults no subsystem test showed (Sections~\ref{sec:res-integration} and~\ref{sec:disc-power}), above all the under-voltage from powering the LTE modem from the Raspberry Pi. The work has reached a prototype run on the vehicle, short of the demonstration in a relevant environment set as the target. 

\paragraph{Limits of the evidence.} The results come from one vehicle, one bench set-up and, at the time of writing, one road session without a speed reference. No reference accelerometer or measured road profile was available, so road accuracy rests on repeatability (Section~\ref{sec:requirements-mapping}), and no driver's glances were measured, so nothing is claimed about distraction. Chapter~\ref{chap:recs} sets out the remaining work.
