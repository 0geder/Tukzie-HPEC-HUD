\chapter{Conclusions}
\label{chap:conclusions}

The project set out to give the TUKZIE Rev~0 an embedded telemetry unit that measures the trike's response to the road and its own powertrain, warns the driver of hazards on the existing dashboard, and keeps its data through gaps in coverage (Chapter~\ref{chap:intro}). The approach was engineering design research along a V-model: thirteen requirements with criteria fixed before each test, bench tests for each part, then vehicle tests for what only the vehicle can show (Chapter~\ref{chap:method}). The research questions are answered first, from the evidence of Chapter~\ref{chap:results}, followed by the requirements and deliverables, the contribution and the limits of the evidence.

\section{Answers to the research questions}
\label{sec:conc-rqs}

\paragraph{RQ1: accuracy and repeatability of the dynamic-response measurement.} The measurement chain is sound on the bench (Sections~\ref{sec:res-acq} and~\ref{sec:res-cal}). Both IMUs were read at 200\,Hz with no queue overflows while every other subsystem ran, but the once-a-minute flash write interrupts reading for up to 44\,ms, and the catch-up reads that follow are new readings, not the missed instants. Calibration brought both to 9.80 to 9.81\,m/s\textsuperscript{2}, but only in the orientation calibrated: with the bench factors the parked vehicle read 10.04 and 10.34. The on-board features pass their self-test at every boot. On the first road session the drop count stayed at zero, but one stall of about 2\,s lost some 400 samples that the count does not show (Section~\ref{sec:res-road1}); Section~\ref{sec:disc-acq} discusses what such loss costs. RQ1 is therefore partly answered: the instrument's rate, calibration and features are verified on the bench, and on the road it repeats over whole passes at one speed but not place by place (ICC 0.16, at most 0.32 in sensitivity checks), as given below.

\paragraph{RQ2: presenting safety-critical information on the existing dashboard.} A sparse presentation, following the glance and clutter limits of Section~\ref{sec:lit-display}, was built on the vehicle's own dashboard. A camera page shows the live view and active alerts, a Sensors page shows distances and fused hazards, and a banner over every page shows an immediate hazard. Only live data are shown: a record more than 3\,s old stops being passed on and is shown as absent within a further 2.5\,s, though individual fields are not aged separately. The delay from an object's appearance to the screen and the driver's glance time were not measured. RQ2 is answered (Sections~\ref{sec:res-display} and~\ref{sec:disc-display}) for the design and its legibility, which puts the warnings' text below ISO~15008, but not for its effect on the driver.

\paragraph{RQ3: latency, data loss and recovery.} On the bench (Section~\ref{sec:res-telemetry}), acquisition loses no queued samples, but the once-a-minute flash write leaves a gap of 33 to 44\,ms that is not recovered. The camera returns a result a median of about 120\,ms after readout on the Raspberry Pi~4 with the whole system running, and 51 to 54\,ms on the Raspberry Pi~5. The unit reconnects about 5\,s after coverage returns, and after a 10-minute outage the queued records were replayed automatically and every logged record was stored once, 24 of them only after a backfill from the unit's log, the store-and-forward behaviour Section~\ref{sec:lit-sync} calls for. On the vehicle the USB link lost 38\,\% of the per-second records on 19 October; the wired serial link that replaced it carried 99.9\,\% on 27 October, when LTE delivered 153 records, 116 of them replayed, a median of 0.44\,s after stamping (n = 9), until the link failed to reconnect from 11:51. RQ3 is partly answered: latencies and recovery are measured on the bench, but latency on the moving vehicle rests on nine records, and on the road the backlog replayed until 11:51, after which the link did not reconnect.

\paragraph{RQ4: features that separate road from powertrain vibration.} The separation follows the approach of Section~\ref{sec:lit-signal-processing}. The time-domain features are verified on the bench. The frequency-domain analysis is built and tested on synthetic signals: it finds the powertrain lines in stand runs and their share of the road spectrum in powered and coasting passes. The stand runs of 23 October put the powertrain's energy between about 40 and 70\,Hz at the rear, with the same lines at the front (Sections~\ref{sec:res-stands} and~\ref{sec:disc-ride}). Its share of the road spectrum is given below.

On the road on 27 October, five passes at 10\,km/h on a smooth loop repeated within a CV of 5.0 (rear) and 5.3\,\% (front) in $a_w$, inside the 10\,\% criterion, and a rougher loop read about twice as high at the same speed; $I_v$ rose with speed, and too few passes were driven at 15 and 20\,km/h for their repeatability. For RQ4, the powertrain lines held 1 to 3\,\% of the road spectrum and coasting did not lower $a_w$, so at these speeds the road dominates the measured vibration.

\section{Requirements and deliverables}
\label{sec:conc-reqs}

The brief asked for three deliverables. The first, the hardware module, was produced with its inertial, battery and position sensing; its head-up display was replaced, by agreement with the supervisors, by integration with the existing dashboard, because a second display would compete for the driver's attention; and its environmental and air-quality payload was not delivered, because the sensor was damaged in wiring (Section~\ref{sec:scope}). The second, the software architecture, reads the inertial sensors at a fixed rate under a deterministic task structure, computes the ride features on board, and logs telemetry locally while sending it to a database over 4G/LTE, replaying what could not be sent. The third deliverable is this report.

The thirteen requirements give a finer measure (Table~\ref{tab:results-summary}). Five are met on the bench: fixed-rate acquisition, calibration, the on-board features, store-and-forward telemetry and the link to the dashboard (R1 to R3, R9 and R10). Seven are met in part (R4 to R8, R11 and R13), mostly for want of a reference or a timed test on the vehicle. On the road on 27 October R10 was met, R1 at the front (0.08\,\% lost in transport at the rear), R4 and R9 in part, and R2 not. The last, the driver's glance time (R12), was not tested, since it needs a participant and an ethics amendment.

\section{Contribution}
\label{sec:conc-contribution}

Against the gap of Section~\ref{sec:lit-synthesis}, the project delivers an integrated, low-cost architecture for a three-wheeled electric vehicle. It combines fixed-rate inertial acquisition with measured timing and loss, on-board ride features, camera detection fused with time-of-flight ranging, cellular telemetry with local logging and replay into a database, and presentation on the existing dashboard. Sensing and a closing-object warning work at both ends. Every part has been measured on the bench except the ranging accuracy, the time to contact and the delay to the screen, and the whole system has run on the vehicle. Integration exposed faults no subsystem test showed (Sections~\ref{sec:res-integration} and~\ref{sec:disc-power}), above all the under-voltage from powering the LTE modem from the Raspberry Pi. The work has reached a prototype run on the vehicle, short of the demonstration in a relevant environment set as the target. 

\section{Limits of the evidence and the way forward}
\label{sec:conc-limits}

The results come from one vehicle, one bench set-up and, at the time of writing, two short low-speed road sessions, the second with the phone's GNSS as the speed reference. No reference accelerometer or measured road profile was available, so on the road only repeatability can be assessed, not accuracy against the true road input (Section~\ref{sec:requirements-mapping}), and no driver's glances were measured, so nothing is claimed about distraction.

This project ends with a working prototype on the vehicle and a measured account of each of its parts. Chapter~\ref{chap:recs} looks ahead: it recommends, from building and testing the system, the steps that would make its data dependable on the road, then suggests further directions and projects for the TUKZIE's sensing.
