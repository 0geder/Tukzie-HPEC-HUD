\chapter{Conclusions}
\label{chap:conclusions}

This project set out to build a low-cost edge-processing telemetry unit for the TUKZIE Rev 0 electric cargo trike and to present its results safely to the driver on the vehicle's existing dashboard. Each research question is answered here from the evidence in Chapter~\ref{chap:results}, separating what has been shown from what depends on the vehicle.

\paragraph{RQ1: accuracy and repeatability of the dynamic-response measurement.} The measurement chain is sound on the bench: two IMUs are sampled at a fixed 200\,Hz with no lost samples, including with every other subsystem running; each is calibrated against gravity, which brought the two from 10.03 and 9.90 to 9.80 to 9.81\,m/s\textsuperscript{2} in the orientation measured; and the features and the dual-sensor fusion are computed correctly. On the first road session (19 October) the chain held its rate on the moving vehicle with no lost samples, and its per-second vibration value separated driving from parked clearly. How repeatably it characterises the trike's response over repeated traversals has not been measured, because that session recorded neither GNSS speed nor raw samples and its runs were not marked. RQ1 is answered for the instrument and for acquisition on the vehicle, but not yet for repeatability.

\paragraph{RQ2: presenting safety-critical information on the existing dashboard.} A sparse presentation was implemented on the vehicle's own dashboard: a camera page with the live view and active alerts, a Sensors page with distances and fused hazards, and a banner over every page for an immediate hazard, all showing only live data and marking missing or out-of-date data as absent. With the second camera, one page switches between front and rear, and the banner labels each warning by side and puts approaching objects first. The touch panel adds no measurable delay, but the time from an object's appearance to the screen has not been measured. Whether a driver can read this within the 2\,s glance guidance has not been measured, so RQ2 is answered for the information hierarchy and update strategy but not for glance time.

\paragraph{RQ3: latency, data loss and recovery.} On the bench, acquisition loses no samples; the only recurring pause, from writing the log to flash, is bounded to one 33 to 44\,ms gap a minute; the camera produces a result a median of about 120\,ms after the sensor is read with the whole system running on the Raspberry Pi~4, and 51 to 54\,ms with detection moved beside the display on the Raspberry Pi~5; every publish succeeded; the unit reconnects about 5\,s after coverage returns; the local log is recovered completely; and after a 10-minute outage the backlog was replayed and every logged record stored once in the database. RQ3 is answered on the bench except for synchronisation latency, which needs GNSS time; the same measures on the moving vehicle remain.

\paragraph{RQ4: features that separate road from powertrain vibration.} The time-domain features are implemented and verified, and the method for separating the two sources, using the motor's Hall pulses as a speed reference, is designed. Without vehicle data and the Hall tap, RQ4 is not answered.

\paragraph{Overall.} The project delivers an integrated, low-cost architecture for a three-wheeled electric vehicle: fixed-rate dual-sensor acquisition with measured timing and loss, on-board ride features, camera detection fused with time-of-flight ranging, cellular telemetry with local logging, automatic reconnection and replay into a database, and presentation on the vehicle's existing dashboard, with front sensing beside the display and rear sensing beside the battery, and a warning for objects closing on the vehicle from either end. Each part has been measured on the bench, and integrating them exposed faults that no subsystem test showed, the most important being the under-voltage caused by powering the LTE modem from the Raspberry Pi, which recurred, with overheating, once the computer was enclosed. The work has reached a working bench prototype rather than the demonstration in a relevant environment set as the target in Chapter~\ref{chap:intro}; the on-vehicle sessions and the calibrations listed in Chapter~\ref{chap:discussion} are what remain, and Chapter~\ref{chap:recs} sets out the work in order of priority.
