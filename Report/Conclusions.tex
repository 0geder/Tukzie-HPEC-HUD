\chapter{Conclusions}
\label{chap:conclusions}

Each research question is answered here from the evidence in Chapter~\ref{chap:results}, separating what has been shown from what depends on the vehicle.

\paragraph{RQ1: accuracy and repeatability of the dynamic-response measurement.} The measurement chain is sound on the bench: two IMUs (on one board for the bench tests, one per node from 22 October) are sampled at a fixed 200\,Hz with no lost samples while every other subsystem runs; calibration brought both to 9.80 to 9.81\,m/s\textsuperscript{2}, in the orientation calibrated; and the features and fusion are computed correctly. On the first road session no samples were dropped, the rate held except for one stall of about 2\,s, and the per-second vibration value separated driving from parked clearly, but that session had no speed reference, raw samples or run markers. RQ1 is answered for the instrument and for acquisition on the vehicle; the repeatability analysis is built and tested, and its answer rests on the second session.

\paragraph{RQ2: presenting safety-critical information on the existing dashboard.} A sparse presentation was implemented on the vehicle's own dashboard: a camera page with the live view and active alerts, a Sensors page with distances and fused hazards, and a banner over every page for an immediate hazard, all showing only live data and marking missing or out-of-date data as absent. One page switches between front and rear, and the banner labels each warning by side and puts approaching objects first. The touch panel adds no measurable delay. The time from an object's appearance to the screen and the driver's glance time were not measured, so RQ2 is answered for the information hierarchy and update strategy but not for glance time.

\paragraph{RQ3: latency, data loss and recovery.} On the bench, acquisition loses no samples and pauses only at the once-a-minute flash write (33 to 44\,ms); the camera returns a result a median of about 120\,ms after readout on the Raspberry Pi~4 with the whole system running and 51 to 54\,ms on the Raspberry Pi~5; the unit reconnects about 5\,s after coverage returns; and after a 10-minute outage every logged record was stored once in the database. On the vehicle the USB link to the recorder lost 38\,\% of the per-second records, which led to the wired serial link. RQ3 is answered on the bench; latency to the database and completeness on the moving vehicle were not measured, because the modem has had no network since 21 October.

\paragraph{RQ4: features that separate road from powertrain vibration.} The time-domain features are verified on the bench, and the frequency-domain analysis (powertrain lines from stand runs at three motor settings, and their share of the road spectrum in powered and coasting passes) is built and tested on synthetic signals. The first session had no raw samples, so RQ4 rests on the second.

\outstanding{from run 2 (road part, 26 October): RQ1 answered with the CV and ICC of the per-pass weighted acceleration over repeated passes and the speed dependence of $I_v$; RQ4 answered with the powertrain lines found on the stands and their share of the road spectrum.}

\paragraph{Overall.} The project delivers an integrated, low-cost architecture for a three-wheeled electric vehicle: fixed-rate inertial acquisition with measured timing and loss, on-board ride features, camera detection fused with time-of-flight ranging, cellular telemetry with local logging and replay into a database, and presentation on the existing dashboard, with sensing and a closing-object warning at both ends. Each part has been measured on the bench and the whole system has run on the vehicle. Integration exposed faults no subsystem test showed, above all the under-voltage from powering the LTE modem from the Raspberry Pi. The work has reached a prototype run on the vehicle, short of the demonstration in a relevant environment set as the target; Chapter~\ref{chap:recs} sets out the remaining work.
