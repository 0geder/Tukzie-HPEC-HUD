\chapter{Recommendations and Future Work}
\label{chap:recs}

This chapter looks beyond the project in two parts. Section~\ref{sec:recs-next} gives recommendations drawn from building and testing this system: what should be done next so that its data can be relied on. Each answers a shortfall found in Chapters~\ref{chap:results} and~\ref{chap:discussion} and names its section; those on which the validity of vehicle data depends come first. Section~\ref{sec:recs-future} then suggests future work: directions and projects that go beyond what this project set out to do. Section~\ref{sec:recs-handover} describes how the work is handed on.

\section{Recommendations from this work}
\label{sec:recs-next}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Acquisition (Sections~\ref{sec:res-acq} and~\ref{sec:disc-acq}). Remove the flash-write pause rather than reduce it, by logging on the Raspberry Pi or reading the IMUs' hardware FIFOs, and set the accelerometer range from the peaks recorded on the vehicle.
\item Calibration (Sections~\ref{sec:res-cal} and~\ref{sec:res-hazard}). Recalibrate both IMUs in place before every campaign and move to a six-position per-axis calibration; calibrate each camera's focal length with a checkerboard; measure the side sensors' bearings on the pods; and confirm the ISO~2631-1 weighting against the standard itself.
\item Power (Sections~\ref{sec:res-integration} and~\ref{sec:disc-power}). Supply the telemetry unit from a regulated vehicle converter, never a Raspberry Pi's USB port or a bare LiPo, and measure each unit's current inline to replace the estimates in the power budget.
\item Synchronisation (Section~\ref{sec:res-hazard}). Fit locking connectors on the sync lines, and measure each node's clock drift and each camera's delay (with the LED method) in every campaign, with chrony running between the Pis.
\item Connectivity (Sections~\ref{sec:res-telemetry} and~\ref{sec:disc-data}). Measure latency to the database and completeness on the moving vehicle; move to an authenticated, encrypted broker that queues for a persistent subscriber, so that completeness no longer depends on reading the log back; and add over-the-air update and log rotation.
\item Display (Sections~\ref{sec:res-display} and~\ref{sec:disc-display}). Enlarge the warnings to at least the ISO~15008 character height at the measured eye distance; mark each field stale by its own age; make the navigation buttons act on press; then measure single-glance durations to the banner in a low-risk trial under an ethics amendment, against the 2\,s limit.
\item Ride analysis (Sections~\ref{sec:res-stands} and~\ref{sec:disc-ride}). Tap one motor Hall line through the optocoupler and count the pole pairs, so that the powertrain lines are followed continuously while driving. For a place-by-place ICC, drive marked straight sections with side video, three passes per speed.
\item Approaching objects (Sections~\ref{sec:res-hazard} and~\ref{sec:disc-hazard}). Set the 2 and 4\,s thresholds from the measured time-to-contact error and the stopping distance, and add a car approaching the parked trike in a closed car park. Label a random sample of at least 200 road alerts against forward video, with faces and number plates blurred before storage, and report precision and false alerts per kilometre.
\item Detection (Sections~\ref{sec:res-hazard} and~\ref{sec:lit-detection}). Compile the detector for the Raspberry Pi~5's Hailo-8 accelerator and measure the gain; compare YOLO11n with the current detector; check daylight colour with an infrared-cut camera; and score the pothole model on its held-out tiles and on the vehicle's camera before it raises any alert.
\item Sensing still to fit (Sections~\ref{sec:lit-proximity} and~\ref{sec:res-hazard}). Replace the 1.2\,m stand-in sensors with the VL53L5CX, then radar or lidar, so that warnings at road speed do not rest on the camera alone; and fit a replacement SEN55 on the Raspberry Pi~4's I\textsuperscript{2}C bus with its connector cable, never wires soldered to test pads.
\item Enclosures (Section~\ref{sec:res-integration}). Log both computers' temperatures in the vented boxes over a full session, and test the printed display mounts before replacing the temporary ones.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Suggestions for future work}
\label{sec:recs-future}

The directions below go beyond this project's objectives. Each builds on the system as it stands and could be taken up by the programme's team or by a future student project.

\subsection{Towards autonomous operation}
\label{sec:recs-autonomy}

By the SAE taxonomy \cite{saej3016} the system is at level 0: it warns and controls nothing. The parts an automated vehicle needs are present in a first form: two cameras with ranging at both ends, time to contact, inertial and battery state, time-stamped logging, on-board processors and a wired network. Each step below builds the evidence the next needs, and the programme's 2026/2027 vacation work on drive-by-wire control and on evaluating and improving this sensing testbed starts along this path \cite{tukzie2026vac}.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Validated driver assistance (still level 0): the alert precision and time-to-contact tests above, and a test that an approaching object is on a collision course, so that parked cars ahead do not raise warnings.
\item Knowing where the vehicle is and how it moves: add wheel speed (Section~\ref{sec:hall-tap}) and fuse it with the IMUs and GNSS into one estimate of position and motion in the vehicle's axes, with every unit on one time base.
\item A dataset from the vehicle: synchronised camera, inertial, position and battery data from the TUKZIE's routes, which needs a revised ethics application because this project stores no frames.
\item Control in a closed area, first at low speed in a depot yard. This needs a drive-by-wire interface, an emergency stop independent of the computers, and a safety case to the functional-safety and intended-function standards \cite{iso26262, iso21448}, with the control path on the deterministic microcontroller side of the split used here.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Fleet-scale road and battery monitoring}
\label{sec:recs-bigger}

Records from many vehicles, through the same store-and-forward link (Section~\ref{sec:db-sync}), would let an operator follow battery health, find faults from abnormal current or vibration, and map road condition from position-tagged detections on streets driven daily; the parts are low-cost and nothing is specific to this trike. Privacy has to be designed in (Section~\ref{sec:ethics}), and none of this holds until the repeatability that RQ1 asks about is shown on the road. That is the gap of Section~\ref{sec:lit-synthesis} at fleet scale: integration on a low-cost trike, measured.

\subsection{Further projects}

\begin{itemize}
\item A road-condition map of the TUKZIE's daily routes from position-tagged vibration, checked against a section whose profile has been surveyed, so that the index of this work is tied to a reference.
\item A glance-time study of the dashboard's warnings with drivers, under ethics approval, against the 2\,s single-glance limit (R12).
\item Battery-health and range estimation from months of BMS current, voltage and temperature records.
\item Radar or solid-state lidar compared with the camera and time-of-flight pair for warnings at road speed.
\item A labelled dataset of road hazards on Cape Town streets (potholes, debris, kerbs) for training and testing detectors on light vehicles.
\end{itemize}

\section{Handing the project on}
\label{sec:recs-handover}

So the next team need not start again, the repository keeps the wiring tables (Appendix~\ref{app:wiring}), the deploy and set-up scripts and their order (Section~\ref{sec:maintenance}), the road-test checklist, run sheet and outstanding register (\texttt{Planning/run2-readiness/}), the lab notebook, and the data index with the location and checksum of every recording (Section~\ref{sec:data-availability}).

\section{Closing remarks}
\label{sec:recs-closing}

Building this system showed how much of a light electric vehicle's behaviour can be measured with low-cost parts, and how much of the engineering lies in making them work together on a platform that vibrates, runs on a limited supply and moves in and out of coverage. The author hopes that the system, its data and its record of what failed give the TUKZIE programme a firm place to start.
