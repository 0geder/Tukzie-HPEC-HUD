\chapter{Recommendations}
\label{chap:recs}

The recommendations are the next steps for this system in order of priority, those on which the validity of vehicle data depends first (Section~\ref{sec:recs-next}); a staged path towards autonomous operation (Section~\ref{sec:recs-autonomy}); the wider value of the work (Section~\ref{sec:recs-bigger}); and the hand-over (Section~\ref{sec:recs-handover}).

\section{Next steps for this system}
\label{sec:recs-next}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Acquisition. Remove the flash-write pause rather than reduce it, by logging on the Raspberry Pi or reading the IMUs' hardware FIFOs, and set the accelerometer range from the peaks recorded on the vehicle.
\item Calibration. Recalibrate both IMUs in place before every campaign and move to a six-position per-axis calibration; calibrate each camera's focal length with a checkerboard; measure the side sensors' bearings on the pods; and confirm the ISO~2631-1 weighting against the standard itself.
\item Power. Supply the telemetry unit from a regulated vehicle converter, never a Raspberry Pi's USB port or a bare LiPo, and measure each unit's current inline to replace the estimates in the power budget.
\item Synchronisation. Fit locking connectors on the sync lines, and measure each node's clock drift and each camera's delay (with the LED method) in every campaign, with chrony running between the Pis.
\item Connectivity. Measure latency to the database and completeness on the moving vehicle; move to an authenticated, encrypted broker that queues for a persistent subscriber, so that completeness no longer depends on reading the log back; and add over-the-air update and log rotation.
\item Display. Enlarge the warnings to at least the ISO~15008 character height at the measured eye distance; mark each field stale by its own age; make the navigation buttons act on press; then measure single-glance durations to the banner in a low-risk trial under an ethics amendment, against the 2\,s limit.
\item Ride analysis. Tap one motor Hall line through the optocoupler and count the pole pairs, so that the powertrain lines are followed continuously while driving.
\item Approaching objects. Set the 2 and 4\,s thresholds from the measured time-to-contact error and the stopping distance, and add a car approaching the parked trike in a closed car park. Label a random sample of at least 200 road alerts against forward video, under a revised ethics application with faces and number plates removed before storage, and report precision and false alerts per kilometre.
\item Detection. Compile the detector for the Raspberry Pi~5's Hailo-8 accelerator and measure the gain; compare YOLO11n with the current detector; check daylight colour with an infrared-cut camera; and score the pothole model on its held-out tiles and on the vehicle's camera before it raises any alert.
\item Sensing still to fit. Replace the 1.2\,m stand-in sensors with the VL53L5CX, then radar or lidar, so that warnings at road speed do not rest on the camera alone; fit a replacement SEN55 with its JST GH cable (GHR-06V-S) on the Raspberry Pi~4's I\textsuperscript{2}C bus, allowing 1 to 6\,h of warm-up for its gas indices; and use connector-based cables, never wires soldered to test pads.
\item Enclosures. Log both computers' temperatures in the vented boxes over a full session, and print and test the dovetail coupon, display shell and bar clamp before replacing the temporary mounts.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Towards autonomous operation}
\label{sec:recs-autonomy}

By the SAE taxonomy \cite{saej3016} the system is at level 0: it warns and controls nothing. The parts an automated vehicle needs are present in a first form: two cameras with ranging at both ends, time to contact, inertial and battery state, time-stamped logging, on-board processors and a wired network. Each step below builds the evidence the next needs.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Validated driver assistance (still level 0): the alert precision and time-to-contact tests above, and a test that an approaching object is on a collision course, so that parked cars ahead do not raise warnings.
\item Knowing where the vehicle is and how it moves: add wheel speed (Section~\ref{sec:hall-tap}) and fuse it with the IMUs and GNSS into one estimate of position and motion in the vehicle's axes, with every unit on one time base.
\item A dataset from the vehicle: synchronised camera, inertial, position and battery data from the TUKZIE's routes, which needs a revised ethics application because this project stores no frames.
\item Control in a closed area, first at low speed in a depot yard, a narrow operating domain in the sense of the taxonomy \cite{saej3016}. This needs a drive-by-wire interface, an emergency stop independent of the computers, and a safety case built to the functional-safety standard \cite{iso26262} and the standard on the safety of the intended function \cite{iso21448}, with the control path on the deterministic microcontroller side of the split used here.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The bigger picture}
\label{sec:recs-bigger}

The same record from many vehicles would let an operator follow battery health, spot faults from abnormal current or vibration and plan maintenance from data, through the store-and-forward link and database (Section~\ref{sec:db-sync}). Pothole detections tagged with position, from vehicles that drive the same streets daily, would map road condition across a city, and measured loads and current on real routes are what the next vehicle revision needs. The parts are low-cost and local, the software open, and nothing is specific to this trike. Two cautions apply: road data include position and camera views, so privacy has to be designed in (Section~\ref{sec:ethics}); and none of this can be claimed before the repeatability that RQ1 asks about is shown on the road.

\section{Handing the project on}
\label{sec:recs-handover}

To let the next student or the programme's team continue without starting again, the repository keeps the wiring tables (Appendix~\ref{app:wiring}), the deploy and set-up scripts and their order (Section~\ref{sec:maintenance}), the road-test checklist, run sheet and outstanding register (\texttt{Planning/run2-readiness/}), the lab notebook, and the data index with the location and checksum of every recording (Section~\ref{sec:data-availability}).
