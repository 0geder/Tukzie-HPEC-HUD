\chapter{Recommendations}
\label{chap:recs}

The recommendations are the next steps for this system, in order of priority, the first being those on which the validity of vehicle data depends (Section~\ref{sec:recs-next}); a staged path towards autonomous operation, the programme's aim (Section~\ref{sec:recs-autonomy}); the wider value of the work (Section~\ref{sec:recs-bigger}); and what the next person needs (Section~\ref{sec:recs-handover}).

\section{Next steps for this system}
\label{sec:recs-next}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Acquisition integrity. Remove the flash-write pause rather than reduce it: log on the Raspberry Pi or separate storage, place the acquisition code in instruction RAM, or read the IMUs through their hardware FIFOs. Set the accelerometer range from the peaks recorded on the vehicle, and bench-test the automatic IMU re-initialisation added in v0.7.7 (on both nodes in v0.7.8), so that a sensor recovers after a bus failure without a reset.
\item Calibration. Recalibrate both IMUs in their mounted position before every campaign and replace the single magnitude factor with a six-position per-axis calibration. Calibrate each camera's focal length with a checkerboard, check each time-of-flight sensor and the fused distance against a tape (C7, C9), measure the side sensors' bearings on the pods, confirm the GNSS speed units against a known speed, and read the ISO~2631-1 weighting parameters against a copy of the standard before citing weighted results.
\item Power. Give the telemetry unit a regulated supply from the vehicle, never a Raspberry Pi's USB port or a bare LiPo; and measure each unit's current inline, and the pack's nominal capacity from the BMS, to replace the estimates in the power budget.
\item Synchronisation. Replace the jumpers on the sync lines with locking connectors; measure the clock offset and drift between each ESP32 and its Pi from simultaneous edge logs, and the camera's own delay optically with an LED driven from the sync pin; and run chrony between the Pis in every session.
\item Connectivity. Check the LTE antenna (the modem has reported no network since 21 October), confirm on a payload with a GNSS fix that the published record carries no position, then measure latency to the database and completeness on the moving vehicle. Move to an authenticated, encrypted broker that queues for a persistent subscriber, so that completeness no longer depends on reading the log back, and add over-the-air update and log rotation rather than truncation.
\item Display. Mark each field stale by its own age (for example the battery state when the BMS stops reporting); make the navigation buttons act on press; measure paint times on the Raspberry Pi~5, alert onset to the screen with a 240\,frames/s video, and the legibility of each page in daylight against the ISO~15008 character height at the measured eye distance; and measure single-glance durations to the banner in a controlled low-risk trial under an ethics amendment, against the 2\,s limit.
\item Ride analysis. Tap one motor Hall line through the optocoupler after measuring its voltage, count the pole pairs by turning the wheel by hand, and use the motor speed to follow the powertrain lines continuously while driving.
\item Approaching objects. Run the time-to-contact accuracy test (C11) with a person walking towards each camera between tape marks, and a car approaching the parked trike in a closed car park, before setting the 2 and 4\,s thresholds from the measured error and the stopping distance. Label a random sample of at least 200 road alerts against forward video, under a revised ethics application covering images of public roads with faces and number plates removed before storage, and report precision and false alerts per kilometre.
\item Detection hardware and models. Enable the Raspberry Pi~5's PCIe connector and compile the detector for its Hailo-8 accelerator, measuring the gain before relying on it; compare YOLO11n with the current detector on frame rate and alert onset; and measure detection confidence with and without the camera's colour correction in daylight, or fit a camera with an infrared-cut filter. Score the pothole model on its held-out test tiles and on the vehicle's camera before it raises any alert.
\item Sensing still to fit. Replace the 1.2\,m stand-in sensors with the VL53L5CX, then radar or lidar, so that warnings at road speed do not rest on the camera alone. Fit a replacement SEN55 with its six-way JST GH cable (GHR-06V-S), which the Raspberry Pi~4's existing I\textsuperscript{2}C bus can read with no other hardware change; its gas indices need 1 to 6\,h of warm-up, so short drives give indicative values only. Fit connector-based cables, never wires soldered to a sensor's test pads.
\item Enclosures. Measure both computers' temperatures in the vented boxes over a full session, and print and test the dovetail coupon, the display shell and the bar clamp before replacing the temporary mounts.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Towards autonomous operation}
\label{sec:recs-autonomy}

By the SAE taxonomy \cite{saej3016} the system is at level 0: it warns and controls nothing. The parts an automated vehicle needs are present in a first form: two cameras with ranging at both ends, time to contact, inertial and battery state, time-stamped logging, on-board processors and a wired network. Each step below builds the evidence the next needs.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Validated driver assistance (still level 0): the alert precision and time-to-contact tests above, and a test that an approaching object is on a collision course, so that parked cars ahead do not raise warnings.
\item Knowing where the vehicle is and how it moves: add wheel speed (Section~\ref{sec:hall-tap}) and fuse it with the IMUs and GNSS into one estimate of position and motion in the vehicle's axes, with every unit on one time base.
\item A dataset from the vehicle: synchronised camera, inertial, position and battery data from the TUKZIE's routes, which needs a revised ethics application because this project stores no frames.
\item Control in a closed area, first at low speed in a depot yard, a narrow operating domain in the sense of the taxonomy \cite{saej3016}. This needs a drive-by-wire interface, an emergency stop independent of the computers, and a safety case built to the functional-safety standard \cite{iso26262} and the standard on the safety of the intended function \cite{iso21448}, with the control path on the deterministic microcontroller side of the split used here.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The bigger picture}
\label{sec:recs-bigger}

The same record from many vehicles would let an operator follow battery health, spot faults from abnormal current or vibration and plan maintenance from data, through the store-and-forward link and database (Section~\ref{sec:db-sync}). Pothole detections tagged with position, from vehicles that drive the same streets daily, would map road condition across a city, and measured loads and current on real routes are what the next vehicle revision needs. The parts are low-cost and local, the software open, and nothing is specific to this trike. Two cautions apply: road data include position and camera views, so privacy has to be designed in (Section~\ref{sec:ethics}); and none of this can be claimed before the repeatability that RQ1 asks about is shown on the road.

\section{Handing the project on}
\label{sec:recs-handover}

To let the next student or the programme's team continue without starting again, the repository keeps the wiring tables (Appendix~\ref{app:wiring}), the deploy and set-up scripts and their order (Section~\ref{sec:maintenance}), the road-test checklist, run sheet and outstanding register (\texttt{Planning/run2-readiness/}), the lab notebook, and the data index with the location and checksum of every recording (Section~\ref{sec:data-availability}).
